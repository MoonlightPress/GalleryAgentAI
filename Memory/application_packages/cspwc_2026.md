## Medium Requirement (Strict)

**Transparent watercolour only.** This is enforced. The following are excluded:

- Gouache (opaque white is not permitted)
- Mixed media with non-watercolour materials
- Watercolour pencil as a primary medium
- Acrylic, oil, or any opaque paint

GEGYjiji's primary practice in transparent watercolor is a direct medium match. No adjustment needed. However: if any works include white gouache highlights (a common technique), those works should not be submitted. Select paintings that use only transparent layers and reserve whites in the paper.

## Work Selection

Submit 2 works. The selection criteria:

**Primary filter — medium purity:** Must be transparent watercolour only, no opaque white.

**Secondary filter — exhibition context:** CSPWC is a traditional watercolour society with an international scope. Jurors value technical command of the medium alongside compositional strength. The work does not need to be traditionally representational — contemporary and atmospheric approaches are accepted — but it should demonstrate genuine control of watercolour transparency as a medium.

**Recommended bodies of work for this submission:**

*Option 1: Two works from "Urban Atmosphere"*  
Tokyo streetscapes or architectural fragments that show the medium's capacity for atmospheric rendering — the specific grey-green light of a rainy street, the layered depth of an urban corner. These translate clearly to an international jury that may not know Tokyo but will recognize skilled atmospheric painting.

*Option 2: One "Urban Atmosphere" + one "Tokyo Still Life"*  
The combination demonstrates range within a coherent sensibility. The still life (interior light, a window, objects on a surface) shows control at intimate scale; the urban work shows the same approach applied to external space.

**Avoid for this submission:**
- Works where cats are the primary subject (too illustrative for this context)
- Works with experimental or unusual formats
- Anything with text, collage, or mixed materials

**Practical guidance:**
- Larger works photograph better for juries — if you have works 38cm × 56cm or larger, prefer those over small studies
- Works completed in 2024–2026 are preferred over older work
- Strong light/shadow contrast and controlled wet-on-wet technique both read well in digital reproduction

## Why This Fits GEGYjiji Specifically

**Exact medium match.** The CSPWC Open Water is one of very few major international exhibitions that is *exclusively* for transparent watercolour. Every other entry is in the same medium. GEGYjiji is not competing against oil painters or printmakers with a "minority medium" penalty. Her practice is the medium this exhibition was built for.

**The atmospheric approach is a strong fit for the juror context.** CSPWC draws jurors from the international contemporary watercolour community — people who understand atmospheric painting, layered transparency, and the capacity of the medium to hold stillness and emotional register. GEGYjiji's approach (quiet observation, atmospheric layering, interior light) is read by these jurors as sophisticated technique, not as an absence of drama.

**International credibility at accessible cost.** CSPWC's 101st annual exhibition is a legitimately prestigious credit. Being selected from an international field and showing in Canada builds the kind of CV entry that matters at Tier 2-3: verifiable, juried, named institution. For CAD $55 and two image files, this is one of the highest potential returns in the current opportunity set.

**The "cross-cultural seeing" angle is genuinely interesting to this audience.** A Chinese watercolourist based in Tokyo submitting Japanese urban scenes to a Canadian juried watercolour exhibition is not a common profile. The work carries a specific perspective that international jurors tend to find compelling — not exoticized, but genuinely different in subject and sensibility from the North American and European submissions they typically see.

**The exhibition timeline creates a useful benchmark.** Acceptance notification comes July 8 — just one week after submission deadline. This is unusually fast feedback. Whether accepted or not, the result comes at a useful moment: early enough in the year to inform the next cycle of applications.

## Draft Artist Statement (for ArtCall submission fields)

ArtCall typically includes an optional artist bio or statement field. The following is written for an international watercolour context — it is different in register from a gallery proposal or a Japanese-market introduction.

**Short bio (50–80 words, for ArtCall bio field):**

```
GEGYjiji is a Chinese watercolour painter based in Tokyo. Her work focuses on 
urban atmosphere, domestic interiors, and the quiet accumulation of light in 
ordinary spaces — a Tokyo apartment, a rain-wet street corner, a window 
through which the city arrives in fragments. She works exclusively in 
transparent watercolour, using the medium's inherent translucency to hold 
stillness and slow observation.
```

**Work description template (for individual work titles in ArtCall):**

For an urban/atmospheric work:
```
Title: [e.g., "Rain Light, Shimokitazawa" or "November Interior, Tokyo"]
Medium: Transparent Watercolour on Paper
Dimensions: [W × H in inches]
Year: 2025 [or 2026]
Price: CAD [amount] [or NFS]
```

Choose titles that locate the work in time and place without over-explaining it. "Rain Light, Shimokitazawa" tells a juror more than "Urban Scene #3" and creates less distance than an abstract title would.
