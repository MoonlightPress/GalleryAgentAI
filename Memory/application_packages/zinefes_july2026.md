## What This Is (Important Distinction)

ZINEフェス is a **booth at a zine fair**, not a gallery submission. Applying means:

1. **Paying for a booth** (via STORES before June 27)
2. **Creating something to sell** at the booth on July 11
3. **Arriving at 9:30 or 10:00 AM** to set up, staying through 17:00, participating in teardown

The application itself is the payment. There is no selection committee, no jury, no proposal required. If you pay, you have a booth.

The harder task is not the application — it is having something worth selling on July 11. See the preparation section below.

## Rules and Eligibility

- Creators must sell directly — no consignment
- Items must be hand-carried via public transit (no advance shipping or delivery to venue)
- Maximum 2 people per booth
- All works must be original and created by the exhibitor
- Prohibited: explicit/erotic content, specific political or religious advocacy
- Refunds not issued after payment

## Recommended Plan: What to Sell

GEGYjiji's work translates directly into several formats appropriate for this context. In rough order of production effort:

**Option A — Small print edition (easiest, lowest risk)**  
Digitally printed reproductions of existing watercolor paintings, A5 or A4, on heavyweight matte or fine art paper. 10–30 copies per image. 3–5 images. Priced ¥800–¥2,500 each. A known print run with a clear price point is the standard ZINEフェス offering and has no stock risk if set conservatively.

**Option B — Artist zine (recommended for first fair)**  
A hand-assembled or professionally printed zine of 12–24 pages: Tokyo interiors, cats, urban atmosphere, light through windows. 8–20 copies. Priced ¥1,000–¥2,000. Can be photocopied (Risograph gives best aesthetic match to the work) or digitally printed. A single coherent body works better than a best-of.

Suggested content sequence for a first zine:
- Theme: "Tokyo Still Life" or "Interior Light" — domestic spaces, windows, cats, ordinary objects
- 10–16 images, hand-lettered or set-type title page
- Short closing note in Japanese and Chinese — not an artist statement, just a sentence or two
- Edition size on the colophon

**Option C — Combination (both prints + a zine)**  
A small zine as the anchor item, supplemented by 2–3 individual prints. Gives buyers different price points. Typical booth for a new exhibitor at this scale.

**Not recommended:** Original paintings (transport risk, too valuable for an attended-all-day format), merchandise that isn't directly tied to the work (tote bags etc. — they don't serve the artist's goals at Tier 1).

## Why This Fits GEGYjiji Specifically

**The format matches the work.** ZINEフェス is populated by artists making quiet, observational work on paper — illustrated zines, photography books, small editions of drawings. Watercolor work about Tokyo interiors and urban atmosphere is native to this context. It is not a translation or a workaround — the fair was built for exactly this kind of work.

**The audience is the right audience.** People who attend ZINEフェス are art book readers and collectors, not tourists. They come specifically to find work they cannot find in a gallery or bookstore. GEGYjiji's subjects — Tokyo's everyday spaces, domestic light, cats — speak directly to a Tokyo-based audience who recognizes these places and has a relationship to them.

**No jury, no gatekeeping.** At this stage, the most important thing is getting work in front of people and learning what resonates. ZINEフェス has no selection committee. The barrier is minimal. The learning — how people respond, what they pick up, what they don't, what questions they ask — is immediate and real.

**The recurring format creates compounding presence.** BOOK CULTURE CLUB runs ZINEフェス monthly/bimonthly throughout the year. A first appearance at the July fair establishes presence that can be continued in September, November, and beyond. The Tokyo art book community is small enough that recurring presence at the same fair is noticed and remembered.

**Cross-cultural position is an asset here.** A Chinese artist making watercolors of Tokyo is not a common profile at Japanese zine fairs. Visitors who pick up the work are picking up something that applies a specific outside sensibility to familiar spaces. That is interesting. It is worth naming — the short note in the zine should acknowledge it lightly.
