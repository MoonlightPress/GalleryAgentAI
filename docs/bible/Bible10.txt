CARD FEEDBACK: THE FOUR ACTIONS
Bible Entry 10 — Written 2026-06-02

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

The opportunity cards in Mochi's page present venues and open calls. Each card
can be read, expanded, and dismissed. But a card that is only read generates no
signal. It leaves the system as ignorant as it was before the artist opened it.

This document defines the four feedback actions that can be taken on any card.
Each action routes to a different companion. Together they close the loop between
what Mochi surfaces and what the system learns from the artist's response to it.

The four actions are:

  FOLLOW       → Saffron tracks the venue long-term
  NOT FOR ME   → Peppercorn learns to filter
  APPLIED      → Submission history is recorded
  MAYBE LATER  → The card moves to the watch list

Nothing in this document is implemented yet. This is the specification.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE PROBLEM A CARD WITHOUT FEEDBACK CREATES

Every card the artist opens is an implicit answer to a question: does this fit?
The artist already knows. She reads the card and she knows — yes, this is
interesting; no, this is not my world; I've already submitted; I'm not ready
yet. That knowledge exists the moment she reads the card. Without a feedback
mechanism, it evaporates. The system resurfaces the same card in a week.

This is not a bug in a narrow sense. The opportunity did not change. The score
did not change. The system has no reason to treat it differently. But the artist
has already made a decision about it, and that decision is more accurate than any
score the pipeline produced. The system is less intelligent than the artist about
her own work. The feedback loop is the mechanism by which that reverses over time.

More specifically: the system currently has no memory of what the artist has
already seen, already dismissed, already tried. The pipeline resurfaces the same
forty opportunities repeatedly. BankART1929 appears in Immediate Best Moves every
time the artist opens the page. It has appeared there since the pipeline first
found it. Whether the artist has already contacted them, already decided they are
not the right fit, already submitted and been declined — the system does not know.

This is the problem the four actions solve.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ACTION ONE: FOLLOW

WHAT IT DOES

The artist taps Follow on a card. The venue is added to Saffron's tracking list.

From that point forward, Saffron monitors the venue independently of the
opportunity pipeline. She watches for:

  - Open call announcements and submission windows
  - Changes in programming direction or curatorial focus
  - Exhibition outcomes for artists in the artist's peer network
  - Indication of what kind of work they have historically accepted
  - How long from first contact to first show for artists at similar career stages
  - Whether the venue accepts unsolicited proposals or works exclusively through
    relationships and referrals

None of this monitoring requires action from the artist. It runs in the background.
Saffron accumulates the intelligence over time. The value grows the longer the
venue is followed.

WHAT SAFFRON DOES WITH FOLLOW DATA

Saffron's venue intelligence is not a set of static facts. It is a model of how
a venue actually works — built from public sources, pipeline data, and whatever
the artist has contributed through her own interactions with the space.

The intelligence takes the form of a venue brief, updated continuously, surfaced
on Saffron's page. It answers the questions that matter before an approach:

  - Is this venue currently active? Do they have a pattern of programming, or
    have they gone quiet?
  - What kind of work do they program? Not their stated mission — their actual
    programming history.
  - What is the realistic path in? Direct submission, referral, relationship
    first, juried selection only?
  - Where is the artist in relation to the venue's typical entry point? Is she
    ready now? Not yet? What would make her ready?

EXAMPLE: BANKART1929

The artist follows BankART1929 in June 2026. She has no relationship with them.
They appear in Immediate Best Moves because they score well on the pipeline's
fit metrics. She is not ready to approach them yet — she knows this — but she
wants the system to keep watching.

Three months later, Saffron's brief reads:

  BankART1929 — followed 3 months

  Programming pattern: 4-6 group exhibitions per year, typically anchored by
  Yokohama artists with 1-2 international or Tokyo-based participants per show.
  Solo shows reserved for artists with prior group show credits at the space or
  at peer institutions (Koganecho, 3331, VACANT).

  Realistic entry path: group show invitation, likely requires existing
  relationship with a Yokohama curator or a warm referral from an artist who
  has shown there. Direct submission has not historically been the path in.

  Current timing: BankART historically announces their April-June program in
  February. If you want to be considered for spring programming, November is
  when relationships need to be active.

  Recommendation: Apply after your next group exhibition credit. The profile
  needs one more institutional show — 3331 or Koganecho would serve this — before
  BankART becomes a realistic target. Follow those venues too.

This is the kind of intelligence a gallerist friend would give you over coffee.
The system builds it from public data, from what the pipeline accumulates, and
from the signal the Follow action produces: this venue matters to this artist.
Pay attention to it.

WHAT FOLLOW IS NOT

Follow is not an expression of intent to apply. The artist may follow a venue
for years before approaching it. She may follow it and decide never to approach
it. Follow means: I want to understand this venue better. It does not commit her
to anything. Saffron watches. The artist decides when and whether to act.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ACTION TWO: NOT FOR ME

WHAT IT DOES

The artist taps Not For Me on a card. The card is immediately removed from her
view and routed to Peppercorn.

Peppercorn records the decision. He does not ask why. Not now. He notes the
venue, the category, the score the pipeline assigned it, and the fact that the
artist declined it without elaboration.

If a pattern emerges — several venues from the same category declined, several
venues with a similar profile declined — Peppercorn surfaces the pattern. Quietly.
Without accusation.

  "You've marked five café gallery opportunities as not for me. Should I stop
   surfacing them?"

The artist can answer yes, no, or say nothing. If she says yes, Peppercorn
adjusts the pipeline weights. That category recedes from Immediate Best Moves.
It does not disappear — she can always navigate to it directly — but it stops
competing for the three daily recommendation slots.

If she says nothing, Peppercorn notes the non-answer. He does not ask again
immediately. He asks again if the pattern continues. He is persistent but
unhurried.

WHAT PEPPERCORN LEARNS

Not For Me is one of two feedback signals Peppercorn uses to refine the pipeline.
The other is explicit preference-setting in his intake questionnaire. The
difference is that the intake is deliberate and periodic, while Not For Me is
continuous and low-friction. Every dismissed card is a data point.

Over time, Peppercorn's model of what the artist does not want becomes as
precise as his model of what she does want. The two models together are more
accurate than either alone. An artist who enthusiastically follows galleries but
marks every fair and market as Not For Me is telling the system something
important about what kind of career she is building. Peppercorn listens.

WHAT NOT FOR ME IS NOT

Not For Me is not permanent. The artist can undo it. Venues she dismissed in
2026 may become relevant in 2028. Peppercorn does not treat a Not For Me as a
lifetime exclusion — he treats it as a preference signal from the artist at a
specific career stage. Tier matters. What is not for me at Tier 1 may become
exactly right at Tier 3.

Peppercorn holds the dismissed venues in a recoverable archive. He does not
resurface them automatically — but if the artist asks to review what she has
set aside, she can. Nothing is deleted.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ACTION THREE: APPLIED

WHAT IT DOES

The artist taps Applied on a card. The system records a submission event: venue,
date, category, and the current state of the artist's career at the time of
submission (career stage, recent credits, materials submitted).

Applied is the entry point to submission history. It is how the system learns
what has actually happened, as opposed to what it has recommended. The pipeline
produces recommendations. Applied produces the historical record of what was
acted on.

WHAT IS TRACKED

  - Date of submission
  - Venue and opportunity type
  - Materials submitted (as the artist reports them: statement, portfolio,
    CV, project proposal — whatever was required)
  - Outcome: pending, accepted, waitlisted, declined, no response

Outcome is not asked at the moment of submission. It is asked later. Peppercorn
notes the submission date and checks back when a response window has passed.
He asks once. If there is no answer, he marks the outcome as unknown and does
not ask again unless the artist initiates.

HOW APPLIED CONNECTS TO QUESTLINES

Applied is the primary source of questline progress. Most questline prerequisites
involve submissions: group exhibition applications, open call entries, residency
proposals, juried competitions. When the artist marks a card as Applied, Peppercorn
checks whether the submission advances any open questline prerequisites.

If BankART1929 is a milestone venue on the "First Institutional Group Show"
questline, and the artist marks BankART as Applied, Peppercorn records the
attempt. If the outcome is acceptance, the prerequisite is met. If the outcome
is decline, the submission is still recorded — it is evidence of activity, not
failure, and it informs how Peppercorn calibrates the timing on a second attempt.

APPLIED AS A CAREER RECORD

Over several years, the Applied history becomes something significant: a
complete record of what the artist tried, when, and what happened. This is
the kind of record most artists maintain poorly or not at all. They remember
acceptances. They forget or suppress rejections. The system maintains the full
picture without judgment.

Saffron uses the Applied history. When she describes what a venue's realistic
entry path looks like, she can compare that path to what the artist has actually
tried. She knows if the artist has already approached a venue and been declined.
She adjusts her venue brief accordingly. "You submitted to BankART in October
2026. They did not respond. This is not unusual — their programming cycle means
unsolicited submissions in that window have a low response rate. The February
window is more active."

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

ACTION FOUR: MAYBE LATER

WHAT IT DOES

The artist taps Maybe Later on a card. The card leaves Immediate Best Moves and
moves to the Watch List section. It does not disappear. It does not get scored
down. It waits.

Maybe Later is the lightest of the four actions. It requires no follow-through.
It is the system equivalent of setting something aside on a desk rather than
filing it or throwing it away.

WHAT THE WATCH LIST IS

The Watch List is a holding area. It contains:

  - Venues and opportunities the artist has explicitly deferred
  - Residencies and institutional targets that are correct in the future but
    not at the current career stage (these arrive via pipeline routing logic,
    not the Maybe Later action)
  - Opportunities with unconfirmed deadlines that the artist wants to monitor

The Watch List is visible on Mochi's page, below the active sections. It is not
prominent. It does not generate Today's Focus actions. It is present so the
artist can see what she has set aside and decide, on her own timeline, whether
any of it has become relevant.

Mochi periodically nudges items from the Watch List back into view. Not urgently —
once per month, for items that have been sitting for more than 60 days. She does
not move them back to Immediate Best Moves automatically. She surfaces them in
a soft prompt: "You set BankART aside 60 days ago. Still not the right time?"
The artist can confirm, move it to Follow, or leave it where it is.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HOW THE FOUR ACTIONS FIT THE THREE COMPANIONS

Each action routes to a different part of the system. The routing is not
arbitrary — it follows the companions' existing roles.

  FOLLOW → SAFFRON
  Saffron's role is to observe and describe the landscape. A followed venue is
  a venue Saffron watches on the artist's behalf. Her venue intelligence is built
  from the Follow list. The longer a venue is followed, the richer the brief.

  NOT FOR ME → PEPPERCORN
  Peppercorn's role is to understand what the artist wants. Not For Me is a
  direct input to that understanding. He is the only companion who can use
  negative preference signals — Saffron describes the world as it is; Mochi acts
  on what is recommended; only Peppercorn can learn what to stop recommending.

  APPLIED → ALL THREE (submission history is shared state)
  A submission event is not owned by one companion. Mochi needs to know not to
  resurface a venue already applied to. Peppercorn needs to advance questline
  prerequisites. Saffron needs to factor application history into her venue briefs.
  Applied writes to shared state. All three companions read it.

  MAYBE LATER → MOCHI (watch list management)
  The watch list is Mochi's holding area. She manages it. She decides when to
  surface deferred items and when to leave them alone. Moving a card to Maybe
  Later is telling Mochi: you found this, and I'm not discarding it, but it's
  not for today.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHY FOUR ACTIONS, NOT MORE

The temptation is to add more feedback states. Love it. Interesting but wrong
medium. Not now but check deadline. Already have a relationship. Wrong city.
Not accepting submissions. Reviewed and declined.

These states are real. They are all things the artist might want to express.
They are not all things the artist will actually express at the moment she is
looking at a card on a Tuesday morning.

The cost of a feedback system is friction. Every additional option is a decision
the artist has to make. Four is already asking a lot. The four actions are
chosen because they map to the four things the artist actually does with a card
she has read: she commits to watching it, she dismisses it, she acts on it, or
she defers it. Everything else is a variant of one of these four.

The system should receive more signal over time, not less. But that signal should
come from the artist doing fewer things, not more. Follow, Not For Me, Applied,
and Maybe Later are the minimum viable feedback system. They are also probably
the right feedback system.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR CODE

The card actions are UI first. They can be added as buttons to the existing
card design. The backend consequences are:

  FOLLOW
  Writes venue ID and follow date to a new file: memory/followed_venues.json
  The Saffron intelligence engine reads from this file. It does not yet exist.
  The pipeline equivalent will be a new step in run_full_mochi_pipeline.py:
  "saffron_venue_intelligence_builder.py" or similar. That step runs on
  followed venues only, not all opportunities.

  NOT FOR ME
  Writes venue ID, category, and timestamp to memory/not_for_me.json.
  The opportunity bucketing logic in api.py reads from this file and excludes
  any venue with a not-for-me record from Immediate Best Moves. The pattern
  detection (5+ dismissals in a category) is a Peppercorn engine function —
  not yet built.

  APPLIED
  Writes a submission event to memory/submission_history.json. This file is
  the authoritative record of what has been applied to. Peppercorn's questline
  engine reads it. Saffron's venue intelligence engine reads it. Mochi's
  bucketing logic reads it to suppress already-applied venues from the active
  sections. The outcome field is updated later, prompted by Peppercorn.

  MAYBE LATER
  Writes venue ID and timestamp to memory/watch_list.json. The api.py bucketing
  logic already routes some items to the Watch List by category. Maybe Later is
  the user-directed version of the same mechanism. The watch list section in the
  UI reads from both sources — pipeline-routed and artist-deferred — and displays
  them together.

The current UI has a "Report" button on each card. That button is the placeholder
for this system. The four actions replace it. The current "Report" state (which
toggles to "✓ Noted") is a visual prototype with no backend consequence. The
four-action system gives it consequence.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A NOTE ON SEQUENCE

The four actions are described here as if they are equally ready to implement.
They are not. The right build order is:

  1. APPLIED first. Submission history is the most concrete and most needed.
     The pipeline already surfaces opportunities. The artist already applies to
     them. The system just does not know. Recording applications closes the most
     consequential gap in the current system.

  2. MAYBE LATER second. The Watch List exists in the UI already as a pipeline-
     routed section. Artist-directed deferral is a small addition to existing
     logic.

  3. NOT FOR ME third. Preference learning is more powerful than deferral but
     requires Peppercorn's pattern detection to be useful. Without the pattern
     detection, it is just a permanent exclude list.

  4. FOLLOW last. Saffron's venue intelligence is the most ambitious of the four
     consequences. It requires a new pipeline engine and a new output format.
     The action itself is simple. What it triggers is not.

Build in this order. The value compounds from left to right, but each step
is independently useful without the subsequent steps. Applied alone is worth
building. All four together are a feedback system that genuinely teaches the
system what the artist needs.
