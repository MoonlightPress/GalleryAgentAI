PEPPERCORN: THE QUESTLINE SYSTEM
Bible Entry 09 — Written 2026-06-01

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT IS

Bible08 defined the three-companion architecture: Mochi the cat, Peppercorn the
mouse, Saffron the bird. It established what each page is for and what each
companion's personality and role are.

This document goes deeper on Peppercorn. Specifically: his second function,
which Bible08 did not fully develop.

Peppercorn has two functions, not one.

The first function is already described in Bible08: the input layer. Peppercorn
is where the artist speaks. She refines her statement, updates her goals, gives
feedback on recommendations, corrects what the system has gotten wrong. Without
Peppercorn, the system is a monologue. He is the feedback loop.

The second function is the subject of this document: the questline tracker. A
structured map of major career goals, broken into concrete milestones, with
progress tracked automatically where data exists and prompted where it does not.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHY QUESTLINES

The system produces daily actions. Mochi presents three things worth doing
today. This is the right level of granularity for a decision about what to do
in the next hour. It is the wrong level of granularity for understanding where
those actions are going.

An artist can follow Mochi's suggestions faithfully for months and still lose
the thread. The submissions accumulate. The connections build slowly. But without
a map of what all this is building toward, the daily actions feel like maintenance
rather than progress. The difference between maintenance and progress is knowing
what you are progressing toward.

Questlines provide that map.

A questline is a structured path toward a major career milestone. It names the
goal, defines the prerequisite conditions, and tracks how many of those conditions
have been met. It does not advise the artist on whether the goal is right for her
— Saffron handles context. It does not tell her what to do today — Mochi handles
that. It shows her where she is on a path she has chosen.

The name is borrowed deliberately from games. In a game, a questline shows you
the complete arc of what you are trying to accomplish, the sub-objectives that
unlock progress, and your current position in the sequence. Careers do not have
experience points, but they do have prerequisites. The questline concept maps
cleanly to the reality of how a career in visual art actually accumulates.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE STRUCTURE OF A QUESTLINE

Each questline has:

  - A NAME: the goal, stated concisely. "Published by a major press." "First
    solo show at a Tokyo gallery." "Accepted to a international juried watercolor
    exhibition." "Japan Watercolor Society member."

  - A COMPLETION CONDITION: what "done" actually means. Usually a single
    concrete event — the acceptance letter, the exhibition opening, the
    publication date. Not a score, not a feeling. An event.

  - PREREQUISITES: the conditions that make the completion condition reachable.
    These are the milestones. They are not arbitrary — they are derived from
    how careers in this specific field actually work, informed by Saffron's
    market data and the curator intelligence the pipeline accumulates.

  - A CURRENT POSITION: how many prerequisites have been met. Which ones are
    still open. Which are in progress.

  - A HORIZON: when this questline is realistic to attempt. Early questlines
    (Tier 1-2) are active now. Later questlines (Tier 3-4) are visible but
    locked — the artist can see them, but Peppercorn does not pressure her
    toward them until the prerequisites from earlier questlines are in place.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

EXAMPLE: THE MAJOR PRESS PATH

This is a real questline, not a hypothetical. It reflects how an artist building
toward publication by a press like torch press actually gets there.

  QUESTLINE: Published by a major art/photo book press

  COMPLETION CONDITION:
    Signed agreement with an independent press for a monograph or artist book

  PREREQUISITES:
    □  10 anthology / group publication credits (zines, journals, artist books)
    □   5 group exhibition credits with documented curatorial framing
    □   2 solo exhibition credits
    □   1 small press monograph or self-published artist book (completed)
    □   Established presence in Tokyo art book ecosystem (TABF, zine fairs)
    □   Active relationship with at least one distributor or stockist (shashasha,
        Dashwood, moom bookshop, etc.)

  CURRENT POSITION (as of first questline setup):
    All prerequisites unmet — questline is newly created. Mochi's daily actions
    will begin generating progress toward the earliest prerequisites.

  HORIZON:
    Tier 3-4. Not active now. Visible. Peppercorn holds this in view so the
    artist understands why the Tier 1 actions (zine fairs, consignment shops)
    are not just ambient presence-building — they are the foundation of this
    questline.

The prerequisite list is not invented. The numbers are informed by what presses
like torch press actually require before they will take a meeting. The pipeline
accumulates curator intelligence. Peppercorn uses it.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

HOW PROGRESS IS TRACKED

Progress tracking operates at two levels: automatic and prompted.

AUTOMATIC TRACKING

Some milestones can be inferred from data the system already has:

  - Exhibition credits: if the pipeline has found and verified a show the artist
    participated in, that credit is recorded automatically
  - Social following: the system tracks follower counts as reported metrics
  - Application count: the pipeline tracks what has been submitted
  - Publication credits: if a verified opportunity has a confirmed outcome,
    that outcome is recorded
  - Deadline dates: the system knows which questline prerequisites have
    approaching windows

Where data exists, Peppercorn updates the questline without asking. The artist
should never have to manually enter something the system already knows.

PROMPTED TRACKING

Some milestones cannot be inferred from pipeline data:

  - The outcome of a submission (accepted / rejected / waitlisted) — the
    system can track that it was submitted but not what happened after
  - The artist's own completed works, works in progress, or bodies of work
    the system does not yet know about
  - Relationships initiated outside the pipeline (in-person introductions,
    word-of-mouth referrals, etc.)
  - Private decisions: deciding to withdraw from a category, change direction,
    abandon a questline

For these, Peppercorn asks. Quietly. One question, not a form. "Did your
submission to the CSPWC Open Water result in an acceptance?" He does not ask
every day. He asks when the context makes it relevant — typically when a deadline
has passed or when a questline milestone is the only thing blocking the next level.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

SOCIAL FOLLOWING AS A TRACKED METRIC

Social following is a real prerequisite for certain questlines and a real
signal about career momentum. Peppercorn tracks it.

Current state (as of 2026-06-01):

  Twitter/X:    90,000 followers
  Instagram:    21,000 followers
  Posts (IG):   35

The disparity between Twitter and Instagram is the kind of pattern Peppercorn
is designed to surface. A visual artist with 90k on Twitter and 21k on Instagram
is carrying an unusual distribution. The gap is not explained by content quality.
It is explained by volume: 35 posts on Instagram means the account has barely
been used. The audience that already exists on Twitter has no equivalent
Instagram presence to follow.

Peppercorn's role here is not to say "you need more followers." It is to say:
you already have an audience. You are not reaching them on the platform most
suited to visual work.

This is an actionable insight with a specific path. Peppercorn surfaces it as:

  INSTAGRAM QUESTLINE (active)
  Current: 21k followers, 35 posts
  Pattern: Twitter audience (90k) not mirrored on Instagram — likely due to
           posting frequency, not content quality
  Milestone: 100 posts (consistency baseline)
  Milestone: 30k followers (parity with expected conversion from Twitter base)
  Next action: Post 3 times this week. Document in-progress work, not just
               finished pieces. Story content — process, materials, the view
               from the window you paint — converts Twitter followers who are
               already interested.

Peppercorn does not frame this as a weakness to correct. He frames it as
an asymmetry that has a clear resolution. The audience is already there.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE THREE COMPANIONS AS A SYSTEM

The companions are not three separate features that share a UI frame. They are
a system with a specific information flow.

SAFFRON SEES THE LANDSCAPE

Saffron's observatory reports what the market looks like: which institutions
are active, what comparable artists are doing, where the opportunities are
clustering, what prerequisites major goals actually require. She does not
advise — she describes.

Her data is the raw material for questline design. When Peppercorn lists the
prerequisites for the Major Press questline, those numbers came from Saffron.
When Peppercorn tells the artist that her Instagram underperformance is unusual
for a visual artist at her follower tier, that context came from Saffron.

PEPPERCORN MAPS THE PATH

Peppercorn translates Saffron's landscape into a set of structured paths. He
takes the reality of how careers accumulate — the prerequisites, the milestones,
the sequencing — and makes it visible as something the artist can navigate.

He also holds the artist's own knowledge of herself: what she wants, what she
does not want, which questlines she has chosen to pursue and which she has set
aside. Without Peppercorn, the system does not know what to optimize for.
With him, every daily action has a place in a larger structure the artist has
approved.

MOCHI WALKS IT ONE STEP AT A TIME

Mochi operates at the level of today. She does not know about questlines
directly — she knows about individual opportunities, actions, and submissions.
But the actions she surfaces are not random. They are informed by which
questline prerequisites are currently unmet and which have approaching windows.

The connection is this: when Mochi tells the artist that today's High Impact
Move is to prepare a submission for the CSPWC Open Water, she is not just
surfacing a high-scoring opportunity. She is surfacing the action that advances
the "International Juried Exhibition Credit" prerequisite of the "Japan
Watercolor Society Member" questline. The artist sees the action. Peppercorn
shows her what it builds toward.

Together the information flow is:

  Saffron sees the landscape
  ↓
  Peppercorn maps the path through it
  ↓
  Mochi walks that path one action at a time

None of the three companions is redundant. Mochi without Peppercorn produces
actions without direction. Peppercorn without Saffron produces paths without
grounding. Saffron without Peppercorn and Mochi produces context without
consequence. The system is complete only when all three are present.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR CODE

Questlines are data, not logic. They are defined as structured objects — a name,
a completion condition, a list of prerequisites each with a type (automatic vs
prompted), a current state, and a horizon tier. They live in memory/ as JSON.

The pipeline populates them. As exhibitions are confirmed, submissions are made,
social metrics are recorded, the questline objects are updated. The update logic
lives in Peppercorn's engine — it reads the latest pipeline output and advances
any prerequisites that can be automatically resolved.

The UI reads the questline objects and renders them as progress structures.
It does not recompute them.

Prompted updates arrive through Peppercorn's input interface: a question,
the artist's answer, a write to the questline object. No inference, no
approximation — just the artist's own account of what happened.

Questlines are never generated automatically. They are authored. When a new
questline is added to the system, it reflects a real decision about how a
career in this specific field actually accumulates. The numbers come from
research. The structure comes from understanding. Neither can be automated.

Social metrics are stored in memory/artist_master_profile.json under a
social_following section. They are updated by the pipeline when available
and prompted by Peppercorn when not. They feed both questline progress
(follower milestones) and Saffron's context layer (market comparison).

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

A NOTE ON TONE

Questlines could easily become a productivity trap. "You have completed 2 of 10
anthology credits. You are 20% of the way to your goal." This framing is wrong.

An artist is not 20% of the way to anything. She is making work. She is making
decisions. She is in the middle of a career that does not reduce to a progress
bar. The questline system exists to help her see the structure of her own
choices, not to measure her against an external standard.

Peppercorn's questline copy does not show percentages. It does not use the word
"progress." It does not compare the artist to a timeline or tell her she is
behind. It shows what has been built and what is still to build — in the same
register that a good map shows terrain. Here is where you are. Here is where
the path goes. That is all.
