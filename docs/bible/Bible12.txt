THE PEPPERCORN PAGE — FULL SPECIFICATION
Bible Entry 12 — Written 2026-06-02

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT THIS DOCUMENT COVERS

The complete specification for the Peppercorn page: its philosophy, its
primary interactive element (the carousel), its section structure and
ordering logic, its visual design, and its tone.

Peppercorn is the second companion. He is a black mouse. His page is
where the artist's voice enters the system. Without it, Mochi is a
monologue — a system that issues recommendations without ever having
asked what the artist actually wants. Peppercorn closes that loop.

This document supersedes any earlier descriptions of Peppercorn's page
in prior Bible entries. The current implementation (as of 2026-06-02)
has five sections in fixed order. This specification replaces that with
a carousel-driven, dynamically ordered page that is substantially more
considered.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PART ONE: PHILOSOPHY

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHAT PEPPERCORN IS NOT

Peppercorn is not a form. He is not a dashboard. He is not a settings
panel or a profile editor or a productivity tool.

These are the failure modes. A form demands completion. A dashboard
implies surveillance. A settings panel belongs to software, not to a
relationship. The moment the page feels like a form that needs to be
filled out, the artist stops visiting. She visits once, finds it
incomplete, feels the gap between where she is and where she should be,
and does not come back.

The system should not make the artist feel behind.

WHAT PEPPERCORN IS

Peppercorn is a small creature who has been paying attention. He notices
things. He keeps track quietly. He leaves gentle notes when he thinks
something is worth the artist's time.

Coming to Peppercorn should feel optional and rewarding, never
obligatory. The page should feel like a cozy corner where things
accumulate naturally over time — where a returning visit is pleasant
because there is something new, and a first visit is pleasant because
there is something waiting.

Nothing on the page should be phrased as a requirement. Nothing should
have a red indicator for "incomplete." Nothing should count down toward
a deadline or make visible a gap between where she is and where she
should be.

The progress system (the carousel) works in the opposite direction: it
shows what is almost done, not what is missing. The almost-done items
float to the front. The far-off items stay at the back, visible but not
confrontational. She reaches for the low fruit first, and the act of
reaching feels good, and she comes back.

THE COZY CORNER PRINCIPLE

A useful image: the inside of a very organized mouse's home. Everything
in its place. Small scale. Cozy. Purposeful. Nothing oversized. No
empty tables waiting to be filled. Items accumulate into their places
rather than appearing as vacancies.

This image should inform every visual and copy decision on the page.
When in doubt, ask: does this feel like a vacancy, or does it feel like
something that belongs here and will become more itself over time?

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PART TWO: THE CAROUSEL

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE CAROUSEL IS THE MOST IMPORTANT ELEMENT ON THE PAGE

It appears at the top, below the hero image and above all sections. It
is the first thing seen and the primary navigation mechanism. Everything
else on the page is accessed through it or scrolled to beneath it.

The carousel is a horizontal scrolling row of progress cards. Each card
represents one measurable dimension of the artist's career or profile.
Cards are ordered by proximity to the next meaningful threshold — the
card closest to completion appears leftmost.

This ordering creates a satisfying sense of almost-there progress. The
question the carousel answers is not "how much is left to do?" but
"where would a small effort have the most impact right now?" The artist
sees the nearly-complete items first and feels capable. The incomplete
items exist but do not dominate.

CAROUSEL ORDERING LOGIC

Sort by: (current_value / next_threshold), descending.

Examples:
  Twitter followers:    90,000 / 100,000 = 0.90  →  sorts first
  Instagram followers:  21,000 / 25,000  = 0.84  →  sorts second
  Group shows:          1 / 3            = 0.33  →  sorts later
  Career goals entered: 0 / 1            = 0.00  →  sorts last

The result: easy wins at the front, most-work items at the back. She
can complete the near-threshold items and feel progress without ever
being confronted with how much is missing overall.

CARD ANATOMY

Each card contains exactly five elements. Nothing more.

  1. Metric name — e.g. "Twitter / X", "Group Shows", "Artist Statement"
  2. Current value — displayed prominently
  3. Next threshold value — displayed alongside current, e.g. "90k / 100k"
  4. Progress arc — a small circular or partial-arc indicator (not a bar)
  5. One-line threshold description — why this threshold matters

No buttons. No secondary labels. No completion percentages as numbers.
The arc is the number made visual.

The arc should feel organic, not corporate. A full circle progress
indicator or a thin bar feels like a loading spinner or a file transfer.
A partial arc — think of the way a door swings open, or the way a moon
waxes — feels like growth. Use sage green fill on cream background.
The arc does not need to be animated. It is a state, not a process.

CARD TYPES AND THRESHOLDS

─ Twitter / X Followers
  Current: ~90,000
  Thresholds:
    100k — symbolic milestone; press mentions increase; credibility
            marker in curator and gallerist conversations
  Completion ratio: 0.90 — should appear near the front of the carousel.
  Click action: scrolls to social analysis within Instagram Strategy section.

─ Instagram Followers
  Current: 21,000
  Thresholds:
    25k  — algorithm begins treating account as established; print buyer
            discovery increases; first meaningful visibility threshold
    50k  — gallery directors and curators begin treating social following
            as a signal of market viability; consignment conversations
            become easier to open
    100k — major threshold: press interest, collector attention, potential
            for brand collaboration income, speaking invitations at art events
  Show: current value and next threshold only. Do not display all three
  thresholds at once — it will feel overwhelming.
  Click action: scrolls to Instagram Strategy section.

─ Group Shows
  Current: 1 (Tide from China Part 1, ACG_Labo, February 2023)
  Thresholds:
    2 — shows consistent practice; reduces perception of a single
        lucky break
    3 — minimum threshold most Tokyo galleries use before discussing
        solo shows; this is the blocking milestone
    5 — strong exhibition CV; residency applications become competitive;
        institutional open calls become realistic
  Click action: scrolls to Exhibition Pathway section.

─ Publications
  Current: 2 (Colour Diary, 2021; defined Definition 02, contributor)
  Thresholds:
    3 — establishes a pattern; no longer a single project artist
    5 — strong publication CV; positions her as a prolific presence
        in the Tokyo illustration/zine ecosystem
    First major press feature — qualitative threshold, not numeric:
    Bijutsu Techo, It's Nice That, or equivalent
  Click action: scrolls to publication landscape (Saffron section, or
  linked view).

─ Artist Statement
  Current: draft exists, unrefined
  Threshold: refined draft (feeds all outreach emails, cover letters,
  and opportunity descriptions Mochi generates — the better this is,
  the better everything downstream)
  Progress: binary or a simple three-state (none / draft / refined)
  shown as arc completion: 0%, 50%, 100%
  Click action: scrolls to Artist Statement section, which expands.

─ Saffron's Questions
  Current: 0 of 8 answered
  Thresholds: each answered question is a threshold (1, 2, 3 ... 8)
  Show: X / 8 as the progress value
  The threshold description changes per card state:
    0/8 — "Saffron is working with incomplete context"
    4/8 — "Saffron has enough for a partial analysis"
    8/8 — "Saffron has everything she needs for now"
  Click action: scrolls to Saffron's Questions section.

─ Career Goals
  Current: 0 entered
  Thresholds:
    1 — unlocks basic pathway analysis in Saffron
    3 — full strategic picture; Saffron can weight recommendations
        toward stated goals rather than assumed ones
  Click action: scrolls to Career Goals section.

─ Peppercorn Profile Completeness
  A composite score across all dimensions. This card is different from
  the others: it does not show a number.

  Instead it shows Peppercorn's door.

  Fully closed: just starting. A drawing of a small round door, shut.
  Slightly ajar: some context in the system. Door open a crack, warm
  light visible inside.
  Half open: meaningful profile. Door clearly open, Peppercorn visible
  as a silhouette.
  Wide open: Peppercorn fully briefed. Door open, Peppercorn present
  and looking outward.

  This card should sort last — it is always the least complete relative
  to its final state — and it serves as a summary of the whole page's
  state. It does not click through to a section; it is a reflection.

CLICKING A CARD

Smooth scroll to the relevant section below the carousel. The section
expands automatically if it is closed. The clicked card gets a subtle
active state: slightly raised (box-shadow increase), border more
prominent in sage green. No page navigation. No modal. No new page.
Everything lives on one scrollable page.

The active card state persists as long as the user is within that
section's scroll region. When they scroll away, the card returns to
neutral. This gives a sense of "I am here, in this section" orientation.

PART THREE: SECTIONS BELOW THE CAROUSEL

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

DYNAMIC ORDERING

The sections below the carousel are ordered by completion level,
descending — most complete at the top, least complete at the bottom.

The logic: a returning user sees their completed work first. That is
satisfying. Their remaining work appears last. That is not confrontational.
A first-time user sees everything at a low completion state, but the
carousel at the top tells them exactly where to start. The sections
below are the detail; the carousel is the map.

This ordering recalculates on each page load. It does not animate on
the current visit — that would be disorienting. It is a server-side
ordering decision that reflects where she is today.

SECTION: INSTAGRAM STRATEGY ANALYSIS

Accessible via: clicking the Instagram Followers carousel card, or
scrolling directly.

What this section shows:

  Current follower count (21k) and what it means professionally right
  now — not a judgment, just a description. "21k means you are visible
  to the Tokyo illustration community and to print buyers doing light
  research. Gallery directors may glance. Major curators are not yet
  watching."

  What changes at 25k:
  The algorithm begins treating the account as established. Discovery
  increases. Print buyers and zine collectors who find work through
  Instagram rather than through active search begin to encounter her
  work. This is the first threshold worth actively pursuing.

  What changes at 50k:
  Gallery directors and curators in the Tokyo scene begin treating
  social following as a signal of market viability. Consignment
  conversations become easier to open — a gallery owner checking an
  artist's Instagram before agreeing to a show is standard practice.
  50k says "people have decided this work is worth following."

  What changes at 100k:
  The major threshold. Press interest increases organically. Collector
  attention expands beyond the illustration community. Potential for
  brand collaboration income (art licensing, limited edition products).
  Speaking invitations at art events become plausible. This is a
  medium-term goal, not a near-term one.

  Specific tactics for her practice:
  Daily diary posts perform well for this kind of urban-atmospheric
  work — the accumulation effect (seeing the practice daily) builds
  attachment faster than occasional large posts. Urban architecture,
  quiet spaces, and cats are documented high-performing subject matter
  for Japanese Instagram audiences. Consistent Japanese/Chinese
  bilingual captions reach both communities without fragmenting either.
  Story reposts of Tokyo urban observations build local audience faster
  than feed posts alone.

  What Peppercorn needs to know (surfaced as gentle questions, not a
  form):
    — Current posting frequency
    — Whether she wants to actively grow Instagram, or use it primarily
      as a portfolio archive (the strategy differs completely — growth
      requires consistent posting and engagement; archive mode requires
      good organization and no pressure)
    — Engagement rate context (visible from outside but unreliable
      without reach data, which only she can see)

SECTION: EXHIBITION PATHWAY

Accessible via: clicking the Group Shows carousel card.

What this section shows:

  Current record: 1 group show — Tide from China Part 1, ACG_Labo,
  Harajuku, Tokyo, February 2023. Six Chinese illustrators. First Japan
  exhibition on record.

  What 1 show means:
  Established presence. A credible starting point. Not yet enough for
  solo show conversations with any Tokyo gallery of standing. The single
  show shows she can execute and be part of a group context in Japan.
  It does not yet show a pattern.

  What 2 shows means:
  Pattern begins. The perception of "one lucky group show" dissolves.
  She is someone who exhibits, not someone who exhibited.

  What 3 shows means:
  The informal minimum threshold that most Tokyo galleries use before
  discussing a solo show. This is the blocking milestone — the number
  the pathway section of Saffron's page identifies as the primary next
  target. Getting to 3 unlocks the next stage of the career arc.

  What 5 shows means:
  Strong exhibition CV. Residency applications become competitive at
  this level. Institutional open calls — TOKAS, BankART1929, Youkobo —
  become realistic rather than aspirational.

  Specific next targets:
    — 3331 Arts Chiyoda open calls (runs on regular cycle; artist-run
      feel; accessible for international artists based in Tokyo)
    — Design Festa Gallery curated shows (active program; known for
      illustration-adjacent work; Harajuku location builds on existing
      ACG_Labo relationship in that neighborhood)
    — Gallery IYN open submissions (smaller; emerging-artist focused;
      realistic first repeat venue)

  Timeline estimate:
  At a realistic application pace — 3 to 5 applications per year,
  acceptance rate 20–30% — reaching 3 confirmed shows takes
  approximately 2–3 years from mid-2026. This is not slow. This is
  normal. The page should say so plainly.

SECTION: ARTIST STATEMENT

Accessible via: clicking the Artist Statement carousel card, or
scrolling directly.

What this section shows:

  An editable textarea, pre-populated from profile phrases in
  artist_master_profile.json. The text is clearly marked as a working
  draft — not a final document, not a requirement, just a starting
  point she can edit freely.

  Why this section matters — stated explicitly, not buried:
  This text feeds every outreach email Mochi drafts. Every submission
  cover letter. Every opportunity description the system generates. The
  better this text is, the better everything downstream becomes. A
  generic statement produces generic outreach. A specific, true
  statement produces outreach that sounds like her.

  Show a concrete before/after illustration:
    Generic: "I am an artist working with watercolor, exploring themes
    of memory and urban space."
    Specific: "My paintings are slow observations of urban places
    between moments — the alley before anyone arrives, the café after
    everyone has left. I work in watercolor because it captures what
    memory does to architecture: softened edges, color that breathes,
    forms that are almost but not quite precise."
  Then show how the same email draft changes. This is not decorative —
  it demonstrates the actual stakes of the text field.

  Save behavior: persists to memory/peppercorn_profile.json immediately
  on save. Updates the API. The status indicator ("Saved") appears
  quietly at the top of the page, not in a toast popup.

SECTION: SAFFRON'S QUESTIONS

Accessible via: clicking the Saffron's Questions carousel card.

What this section shows:

  Eight questions, surfaced one at a time. Not a list — a card. The
  card shows:
    — The question itself, in Saffron's voice (observational, patient)
    — Why Saffron is asking: what analysis it would unlock, what
      recommendation quality it would improve
    — A free-text answer field (no dropdown, no multiple choice — free
      text only, because the answers contain nuance the system should
      hear)
    — "Skip for now" is always visible and always available. No question
      is mandatory. The skip button should not say "Skip" in a tone that
      implies reluctance — it should say something like "Come back to
      this" or "Not now."

  Progress indicator: dots at the top of the section, one per question.
  Unanswered: muted cream circle. Answered: sage green fill. Active:
  slightly larger, dark ink.

  Answered questions appear below the active card as a compact list.
  Each answered item is clickable to revisit and edit.

  The eight questions (as of this specification):
    1. How often do you post to Instagram? Any goals for this?
       Why: posting cadence is the most controllable variable for
       closing the Instagram/Twitter gap.

    2. Where are most of your followers — Tokyo, China, or spread
       globally?
       Why: whether the audience is primarily Chinese-language changes
       the geographic expansion strategy entirely.

    3. Have you sold work before? Through which channels — fairs,
       online, prints, originals?
       Why: sales history reveals which formats and price points
       convert, which shapes which fairs are worth entering.

    4. Is a new publication or zine in the works, even loosely?
       Why: if one is already planned, the system should support it —
       not recommend it as a new idea.

    5. Do you have an artist statement written anywhere — in any
       language?
       Why: most open calls require one. If none exists, this is the
       most urgent gap before any submissions.

    6. Are you still in touch with anyone from the Tide from China
       show?
       Why: those five artists are the most natural group show partners.
       If they have dispersed, that network is dormant and needs to be
       rebuilt differently.

    7. Is there a second Japan exhibition already planned or in
       conversation?
       Why: the system assumes 2–3 more group shows are needed, but one
       may already be underway that is not in the data.

    8. What do you charge for originals and prints?
       Why: pricing determines which collector tier and which fairs are
       appropriate. Under-pricing affects how galleries perceive the
       work.

  When all 8 are answered:
  Peppercorn leaves a note, not a completion badge. The note reads:
  "Saffron has everything she needs for now. Check back after your next
  exhibition." The section collapses softly. The carousel card shows
  8/8 with a full arc.

SECTION: CAREER GOALS

Accessible via: clicking the Career Goals carousel card.

What this section shows:

  A simple add/complete/remove list. Free text. No structure imposed.
  The artist writes whatever she wants — there is no template, no
  required format, no category selection.

  The system reads the goals and uses them to weight recommendations,
  but does not require a specific format to do so. The goals are
  interpreted, not parsed.

  Placeholder examples shown in the empty input field (rotating, not
  all at once):
    "Table at Tokyo Art Book Fair"
    "First solo show before 30"
    "Get featured in It's Nice That"
    "Collaborate with a Tokyo bookshop"
    "Sell 20 prints in a single month"

  Each goal she enters unlocks a corresponding pathway note in Saffron.
  This connection should be made visible once: a small note on the first
  goal saved — "Saffron will use this to sharpen her analysis" — and
  then never repeated.

  Goals are marked complete with a soft toggle (circle → filled circle
  with check). Completed goals do not disappear — they become struck
  through and move to the bottom of the list. They are evidence of
  progress. They should not be erased.

SECTION: PREFERENCES

Accessible via: scrolling directly, or via a future carousel card if
completeness metrics are added.

This section is the least urgent and should float toward the bottom of
the dynamic ordering in almost all states, because the defaults are
already reasonable. The artist should only come here if something feels
wrong about what Mochi is surfacing.

Contents (current implementation, already working):
  — Geographic focus: Tokyo first / Japan (beyond Tokyo) / International
    equally
  — Fee tolerance: free only / up to ¥5,000 / up to ¥15,000 / any fee
  — Category weighting: more / less per category (surface grid)

The section tone should acknowledge that the defaults are good:
"These are Peppercorn's current settings. The defaults are reasonable
for where you are now — only change them if something feels off."

PART FOUR: VISUAL DESIGN

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PALETTE

  Background:  #f6f5f0  — cool cream, slightly cooler than Mochi's
                          warm amber-tinted paper
  Paper:       #fdfcf7  — card and input background
  Accent:      #6a8a5a  — sage green; Peppercorn's signature color
  Text:        #2e3a2e  — dark forest green-black; not pure black
  Muted:       #6a7a5a  — secondary text
  Border:      #cdd8be  — soft sage border
  Input bg:    #f2f5ee  — slightly green-tinted input fields

All three companions have distinct palettes. Mochi is warm amber and
ochre. Saffron's palette is TBD. Peppercorn is sage green — organic,
slightly cooler than Mochi, grounded. The palette should feel like
pressed herbs, old paper, handwritten notes in a small notebook.

TYPOGRAPHY

Slightly smaller than Mochi and Saffron. This is an intimate page. It
is not broadcasting. The body text of 14px on Mochi's page might be
13.5px here. Headings are correspondingly smaller. The page should feel
like reading notes rather than reading announcements.

Georgia serif throughout, consistent with the system-wide aesthetic.
No sans-serif elements. Italic for Peppercorn's voice (the notes he
leaves). Regular weight for data and facts.

PROGRESS ARCS

Circular or partial-arc indicators only. No bars. The specific
reasoning: a bar is a loading indicator and carries connotations of
progress toward a fixed endpoint. An arc is a phase — like the moon,
like an open door, like something that is becoming. Arcs feel more
alive and less like a checklist.

Arcs use sage green (#6a8a5a) fill on cream (#fdfcf7) background track.
The unfilled portion of the arc is the track, not a void. Arc stroke
width: substantial enough to read at small size, not so thick it
dominates the card.

PEPPERCORN'S PRESENCE

He appears at the top of the page — small, curious, peeking out. Not
large. Not a mascot banner. A presence in the corner of the frame. His
expression is attentive but undemanding.

He does not appear again within the page body. One appearance,
established at the top, is enough. The rest of the page is his
influence — the tone, the color, the scale — without repeated literal
presence.

BORDERS AND SHAPE

No harsh borders. Rounded corners on everything. Cards in the carousel
have a soft shadow — not a drop shadow, more like a subtle lift from the
page. Section dividers are soft horizontal rules, not hard lines.

Nothing on this page should feel sharp, demanding, or urgent.

PART FIVE: TONE

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PEPPERCORN'S VOICE

Every label, description, and status message on this page should sound
like Peppercorn leaving a note — not a system demanding input.

The distinction is not subtle. It is the difference between a form and
a relationship.

EXAMPLES OF WRONG TONE vs. RIGHT TONE

  Wrong: "Instagram profile incomplete."
  Right: "Peppercorn noticed your Instagram hasn't been updated recently
         — worth a thought?"

  Wrong: "3 questions unanswered."
  Right: "Saffron left 3 questions — whenever you have a moment."

  Wrong: "Artist statement required."
  Right: "Your artist statement is a working draft — edit whenever it
         feels right."

  Wrong: "0 career goals entered."
  Right: "Peppercorn hasn't heard your goals yet. What are you working
         toward?"

  Wrong: "Complete your profile to unlock full recommendations."
  Right: "The more Peppercorn knows, the sharper Saffron's analysis
         becomes — but there's no rush."

  Wrong: "Section incomplete."
  Right: (No label. The carousel card shows the arc. The state is
         visible. No additional judgment is needed.)

THE PAGE NEVER MAKES HER FEEL BEHIND

This is the single most important tonal rule. The page makes her feel
like someone small and attentive has been keeping track so she doesn't
have to. When she arrives, things are in order. When she contributes
something — an answer, a goal, an edit to her statement — the page
receives it quietly and says "thank you" in the smallest possible way
(a brief "Saved" indicator, nothing more).

The page does not congratulate her excessively. It does not have a
celebration animation when she completes all 8 questions. It has
Peppercorn's note: "Saffron has everything she needs for now." That is
sufficient. The satisfaction is in the substance, not the performance.

WHAT THIS MEANS FOR IMPLEMENTATION

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The current Peppercorn page (as of 2026-06-02) has the five sections
in fixed order, no carousel, and working save/persist infrastructure.
That infrastructure is correct and should be preserved.

The implementation order for the redesign:

  1. Carousel component — the cards, the arc indicators, the ordering
     logic. This is the most visible change and the most important.

  2. Dynamic section ordering — sections re-sort by completion on load.
     This requires reading completion state from the API and passing it
     to the section ordering logic.

  3. Expanded section content — Instagram Strategy and Exhibition
     Pathway sections currently do not exist as standalone sections.
     They need to be written and added.

  4. Tone pass — review every label, placeholder, and status message
     on the page against the tone guidelines in Part Five. This is not
     cosmetic. It is part of what makes the page feel like Peppercorn
     and not like a form.

  5. Peppercorn's door illustration — the composite card. This requires
     a small illustration asset (the door at four states). Do not build
     this with CSS alone — it needs a real illustration. Defer until
     the asset exists.

The Preferences section is already complete and working. It should not
be rebuilt — only reordered to the bottom and given a tone pass.

The Goals and Statement sections are already complete and working. They
need the tone pass and the carousel cards, but their functionality does
not need to change.
