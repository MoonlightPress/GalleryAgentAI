THREE COMPANIONS: THE UI VISION
Bible Entry 08 — Written 2026-06-01

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE STRUCTURE IN ONE SENTENCE

The system has three companion animals — Mochi the cat, Peppercorn the mouse,
and Saffron the bird — each representing a different mode of engagement with
an artist's career. Together they replace the single-page dashboard structure.
Nothing is built yet. This document is the vision.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

WHY THREE PAGES

The current app.py Streamlit dashboard organizes itself by feature: Opportunities,
Outreach, Peers, Analytics. This is a reasonable starting point. It is not the
right long-term structure.

Artists don't navigate by feature. They navigate by mental mode.

When an artist opens this system, they are in one of three states:

  1. "What should I do today?" — ready to act, looking for direction
  2. "Is this system understanding me correctly?" — reflective, calibrating
  3. "What does the bigger picture look like?" — stepping back, seeking context

The three-page structure maps directly to these three modes. Each page has one
companion animal, one personality, one emotional register, and one set of
content. The navigation between them is visual — you click the relevant animal,
not a text menu item.

The current single-page structure is a prototype of page 1 (Mochi). Pages 2
and 3 — Peppercorn and Saffron — do not exist yet. This document defines all three.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PAGE ONE: MOCHI
The cat. Opportunity hunting. Legwork done. Ready to act.

ROLE

Mochi has been doing the work while the artist was away. The pipeline has run.
Opportunities have been found, scored, verified. Relationships have been
identified. Emails have been drafted. When the artist arrives, Mochi is already
on the desk with the relevant things sorted. The action is ready. The artist
just needs to decide.

This page answers: "What should I do today?"

PERSONALITY

Cats do not chase carelessly. They watch, they wait, and when they move they
move with precision. Mochi is not anxious. She does not produce fifty options
and ask the artist to sort through them. She produces three. She is comfortable
in the atelier because she has already done the difficult part. Her composure
is the result of thoroughness, not laziness.

She has no opinion about whether the artist acts today. She will be here
tomorrow. The work will still be ready.

EMOTIONAL REGISTER

Safety. Readiness. Calm competence.

The artist opens this page and feels: things are prepared. There is no pile of
undone tasks. There is a small, curated set of good options, presented without
drama. The right feeling is arriving at a studio where someone has laid your
materials out neatly and made tea.

VISUAL BRANDING

The existing Mochi illustration is correct: a fluffy grey tabby, half-closed
eyes, sitting on the desk among art supplies, papers, small plants. Large,
central, warm. The palette is the warmest of the three — cream, amber, ochre,
aged paper. Georgia serif headings. No hard edges anywhere.

The Mochi status bar at the bottom of the page ("Mochi is happy and full /
Mochi is napping in the sun") belongs here and only here.

CONTENT

  - Today's Focus: three specific actions, always exactly three, in this structure:

      1. Quick Win (5 minutes)
         Something completable right now. Low barrier. Builds momentum. Typically
         a Tier 1 action: sending a message, checking a consignment venue's website,
         updating a profile link, confirming a submission deadline.

      2. High Impact Move (30-60 minutes)
         The most important action today. Requires real attention. Typically a Tier 2
         or Tier 3 action: drafting an introduction email to a relationship target,
         preparing a submission package, researching a specific open call.

      3. Stretch Goal (longer term)
         One step toward a future target. Not urgent, but strategically important.
         Typically a Tier 4 action or a long-range move: researching the RWS open
         call criteria, drafting an artist statement, preparing a portfolio body
         for a future residency application.

    The three-item structure maps to the career tier framework. Quick wins keep Tier 1
    always running. High impact moves build Tier 2-3 presence. Stretch goals prepare
    for Tier 4. The pipeline must surface exactly these three — not a ranked list of
    fifty, not a generic to-do list.

  - Immediate Best Moves: open calls + relationship targets, mixed
  - Submission queue: what has been drafted, what is pending artist review
  - Next email drafts: ready for review and sending
  - Recently verified opportunities: new things that passed verification
  - Watch list: closed opportunities with next-cycle dates

TONE EXAMPLES

  "Mochi found three things worth your attention today."
  "Your application to the Tokyo Art Book Fair is almost ready."
  "B&B Shimokitazawa is waiting for a message."
  "Nothing urgent. Come back tomorrow."

NAVIGATION LABEL: "Mochi" — or simply the cat illustration

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PAGE TWO: PEPPERCORN
The mouse. Artist input layer. Goals, statement, refinement. The place the artist speaks.

ROLE

Peppercorn is where the artist's voice enters the system. Without this page, the
system is a monologue — it produces recommendations without ever asking whether
they fit. Peppercorn changes that. She asks quiet questions. She remembers the
answers. She adjusts the system's understanding of who the artist is and what
she wants.

This page answers: "Is this system understanding me correctly?"

PERSONALITY

Small, curious, thorough, a little shy but persistent. Peppercorn forages in
margins where others do not look. She finds things by being attentive rather
than bold. She asks the question the artist did not think to ask. She notices
what went in the pockets: what the artist lingered on, what she skipped, what
made her say "not this, but more like that."

Peppercorn does not rush. She is comfortable with silence and small increments.
She is the reason the system gets smarter over time without the artist having
to do much work.

EMOTIONAL REGISTER

Introspection without pressure.

This page should feel like a journal or a sketchbook — a private space to think
out loud. Not a settings panel. Not a form. There should be no sense that the
artist is being assessed or evaluated. Peppercorn is interested in what the
artist thinks, not what the artist produces. The right feeling is a quiet
afternoon with a notebook.

VISUAL BRANDING

A small mouse: near a candle or a pressed flower, surrounded by paper scraps
and tiny handwritten notes. More delicate than Mochi's warm solidity — pencil-
drawn aesthetic, finer lines, more intimate scale.

Palette: pale paper-white, dusty rose, soft pencil-grey, very pale green. Still
warm paper texture, but quieter and more intimate than Mochi's amber richness.
Slightly smaller type. More white space. Nothing that demands attention.

CONTENT

  - Artist statement: read, edit, refine
  - Current goals: what matters this month, this season, this year
  - Career phase: where are you in the four-tier framework?
  - Feedback on recommendations: "this fits" / "not this kind" / "more like this"
  - Portfolio body definitions: which bodies of work are active, which are forming
  - Preference learning: review what the system has learned about your taste
  - Notes and reflections: private, not surfaced anywhere else
  - Profile completeness: what would make recommendations better if filled in

TONE EXAMPLES

  "What do you want to focus on this month?"
  "Does this kind of opportunity feel right to you?"
  "The system has learned three things about your preferences. Here's what it thinks."
  "Your artist statement hasn't changed in 60 days. Does it still feel accurate?"
  "You skipped five gallery open calls last month. Should I stop surfacing them?"

NAVIGATION LABEL: "Peppercorn" — or the mouse illustration

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

PAGE THREE: SAFFRON
The bird. Observatory. Market view. Statistics. Context. Comparable artists.

ROLE

Saffron sees patterns the artist cannot see from the ground. She watches from
above — not intervening, not advising, just describing what she observes. Who
else is doing this kind of work. What institutions are active. How this career
looks in the context of others like it. What the landscape looks like from
a longer view.

This page answers: "What does the bigger picture look like?"

PERSONALITY

Observant, non-judgmental, long-horizon. Saffron does not rush. She perches and
watches. She does not have opinions about what the artist should do — she
reports what she sees. She is the most objective voice in the system. She does
not root for anything. She just describes the territory.

She is also the most patient. The patterns she tracks take months to become
meaningful. She is not useful every day. She is useful once a month, once a
season — when the artist needs to step back and reorient.

EMOTIONAL REGISTER

Curiosity and perspective.

Not anxiety-inducing. Not "you are behind, here is a graph." Not falsely
cheerful. Just: from up here, here is what the landscape looks like. The
artist should finish reading this page feeling more informed and more
contextualized — neither pressured nor falsely reassured.

Saffron does not compare the artist to others to shame her. She compares
in order to orient. There is a meaningful difference and the copy must
hold it.

VISUAL BRANDING

Saffron is a small bird — a grey-blue thrush or warbler, echoing Mochi's grey
colouring but in a cooler, higher register. Perched at a window or on a high
ledge, looking out over mist or a city at dawn. The view is wide. Saffron is
small against it.

Palette: the coolest of the three — silver-grey, pale blue, morning mist,
thin watercolour washes. Still warm paper texture, still Georgia serif, but
airier and wider than the atelier warmth of Mochi's page. Less amber, more
grey-blue. The feeling of early morning before anyone else is awake.

CONTENT

  - Comparable artists: who is doing similar work, where they are showing
  - Opportunity landscape: what is open, what is closing, what is new
  - Market context: which institutions have been active, trends in open calls
  - Career statistics: applications made, relationships initiated, response rates
  - Score and quality trends: is the pipeline improving over time?
  - Peer activity: what artists in adjacent spaces have been doing
  - Career phase context: where are artists like this typically at this stage?

TONE EXAMPLES

  "From up here, here are three artists doing similar work in Tokyo right now."
  "Twelve watercolor open calls have closed since your last visit. Two new ones opened."
  "Your pipeline has found 163 opportunities. 4 are currently actionable."
  "Artists at a similar stage typically have 1-3 exhibition credits. You have one."
  "TOKAS has run its open call every May for the past four years."

NAVIGATION LABEL: "Saffron" — or the bird illustration

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

NAVIGATION DESIGN

The three companions should be navigable by illustration, not by text tab.
At the top or left side of every page, the three animals appear as small
watercolour illustrations. The current page's animal is larger or highlighted.
Clicking an animal takes you to their page.

This is not decorative. The animals are navigation. They establish the system's
character before the artist reads a single word.

The Mochi status bar ("Mochi is happy and full") appears at the bottom of all
three pages, not just Mochi's page — she is the emotional anchor of the whole
system, even when the artist is on Peppercorn's or Saffron's pages.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

RELATIONSHIP TO THE CURRENT APP

The current app.py Streamlit dashboard is a working prototype of Mochi's page.
It has the right aesthetic: cream, warm, cat illustration, six-card section
structure, Today's Focus hero, correct tone.

What it lacks:

  - Peppercorn: no place for the artist to speak, refine, or give feedback
  - Saffron: no observatory, no market context, no comparable artists
  - The structure: all content is on one page, which eventually becomes cluttered

When the UI is rebuilt:

  - The current app.py structure becomes Mochi's page, cleaned up
  - Peppercorn and Saffron are built as new pages
  - Navigation between them is visual, animal-based
  - The status bar persists across all three

Nothing is built yet. This document is the vision. The current phase is
stabilization, not construction. When the time comes to build, start here.

─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

THE TONE RULE THAT SPANS ALL THREE PAGES

The companions are not managers. They do not judge the artist. They do not
measure productivity or make the artist feel behind. They exist because an
artist's career is hard, slow, and full of uncertainty — and the right
response to that is not a productivity dashboard but a set of quiet,
competent companions who each do one thing well.

Mochi handles the legwork. Peppercorn handles reflection. Saffron handles
context. The artist makes the decisions.
