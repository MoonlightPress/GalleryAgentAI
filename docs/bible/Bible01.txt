# MOCHI BIBLE

## Volume I — Origins, Purpose, and Philosophy

### What Mochi Is

Mochi is an AI-assisted artist career support system.

It is not a portfolio website.

It is not a CRM.

It is not an opportunity database.

It is not a recommendation engine.

Those things exist inside the project, but none of them are the purpose of the project.

The purpose of the project is to reduce the friction between artists and meaningful career opportunities.

The project exists because modern artists spend enormous amounts of time performing administrative and research tasks that have little to do with making art.

Artists search for galleries.

Artists search for open calls.

Artists search for residencies.

Artists search for grants.

Artists search for publications.

Artists search for collaborators.

Artists search for communities.

Artists search for validation.

Most of this work is repetitive.

Most of this work is emotionally exhausting.

Most of this work is poorly supported by existing software.

Mochi was created to address that problem.

The long-term vision is an Artist Career Operating System.

The project should eventually help an artist determine:

* What should I do today?
* What should I do this week?
* What should I do this month?
* Which opportunities matter?
* Which opportunities do not matter?
* Who should I contact?
* Who should I ignore?
* Where should I invest my energy?

The project is advisory.

Humans remain responsible for all decisions.

The system should never automatically conduct outreach without human review.

---

### Why This Project Exists

The project began with a specific artist.

This is important.

Many systems are designed around hypothetical users.

Mochi was designed around a real artist.

The artist was a watercolor painter.

Her work contained recurring themes:

* urban environments
* architecture
* memory
* absence
* atmosphere
* quiet observation

The project originally existed because the creator wanted to help her.

This is not merely personal history.

It is architectural history.

Many of the most important design decisions emerged because recommendations that appeared reasonable to a machine were obviously wrong when viewed through the lens of a real artist.

The creator cared deeply about the artist.

That emotional investment altered the project's development trajectory.

The project continued evolving long after a simple recommendation engine would have been considered finished.

Because the objective was not simply finding opportunities.

The objective was helping someone navigate a difficult career.

That distinction shaped everything that followed.

---

### The Hidden Problem

At the beginning, it appeared that the problem was information scarcity.

This turned out to be false.

Artists are not suffering from a lack of information.

Artists are drowning in information.

The real problem is prioritization.

The real problem is uncertainty.

The real problem is emotional burden.

The real problem is deciding where to spend limited time and energy.

This realization became one of the most important turning points in the project.

The goal shifted from:

"Find opportunities."

to:

"Help artists decide what to do."

This distinction is subtle but fundamental.

An opportunity database answers:

"What exists?"

A career system answers:

"What matters?"

Mochi increasingly moved toward the second question.

---

### Core Design Principles

Principle 1:

Actionability is more valuable than information.

Principle 2:

Verification is more valuable than ranking.

Principle 3:

Understanding the artist is more valuable than understanding the opportunity.

Principle 4:

Reducing cognitive burden is as important as increasing opportunity discovery.

Principle 5:

Humans remain responsible for final decisions.

Principle 6:

The system should optimize for long-term career growth rather than short-term activity.

Principle 7:

The purpose of the system is not to maximize submissions.

The purpose is to maximize meaningful progress.

---

### The Long-Term Vision

The final form of Mochi is not a search engine.

It is not a dashboard.

It is not a recommendation engine.

The final form resembles an operating system for an artist's professional life.

Potential future capabilities include:

* opportunity discovery
* gallery relationship management
* submission planning
* portfolio guidance
* artist statement guidance
* exhibition strategy
* publication strategy
* networking support
* collaboration support
* daily quests
* weekly goals
* long-term planning

The project should always be evaluated against a single question:

"Does this help the artist decide what to do next?"

If the answer is no, the feature is probably unnecessary.
