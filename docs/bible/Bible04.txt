# MOCHI BIBLE

## Volume IV — Architecture

### Introduction

The architecture of Mochi did not emerge from a top-down design process.

It emerged through repeated confrontation with failure.

Almost every major layer exists because a previous layer proved insufficient.

This means the architecture is historical.

To understand why a layer exists, it is often necessary to understand what problem it was attempting to solve.

The system gradually evolved from:

```text
Discovery Engine
```

into:

```text
Artist Career Intelligence System
```

The architecture reflects that transition.

---

# Discovery Layer

### Purpose

Find opportunities.

This is the oldest layer in the project.

Without discovery there is no database.

Without a database there is nothing to rank.

The discovery layer searches for:

* galleries
* open calls
* contests
* publications
* residencies
* fairs
* organizations

The output is raw opportunity data.

---

### Strengths

The layer can find opportunities at scale.

Discovery is no longer the primary weakness of the project.

Large numbers of opportunities can be collected.

---

### Weaknesses

Discovery alone creates noise.

Discovery is not intelligence.

Discovery does not understand fit.

Discovery does not understand quality.

Discovery does not understand timing.

The project eventually learned:

Finding opportunities is the easiest part.

---

# Ranking Layer

### Purpose

Sort opportunities.

The ranking layer emerged because discovery produced too much information.

The objective was:

```text
What should appear first?
```

---

### Strengths

The layer can identify broad relevance.

It can dramatically reduce search space.

---

### Weaknesses

The layer initially lacked reality checks.

A highly-ranked opportunity was not necessarily useful.

This eventually led to:

* Truth Alignment
* Verification

---

# Artist DNA Layer

### Purpose

Understand the artist.

The Artist DNA Layer changed the direction of the project.

Rather than beginning with opportunities, it begins with the artist.

Inputs include:

* artwork
* visual tendencies
* recurring themes
* medium
* emotional tone

Outputs include:

* artist profile
* thematic profile
* aesthetic profile

---

### Importance

This became one of the strongest systems in the project.

Many later systems depend upon it.

Without Artist DNA, recommendations tend to become generic.

---

# Watercolor Layer

### Origin

The Watercolor Layer exists because of failure.

Specifically:

The Photography Problem.

---

### Purpose

Prevent painters from being overwhelmed by photography opportunities.

The layer increases sensitivity to medium.

It explicitly values:

* painting
* watercolor
* fine art contexts

This was one of the most successful corrective layers.

---

# Source Purity Layer

### Purpose

Distinguish opportunities from noise.

The system discovered that many highly-ranked results were not opportunities.

Examples:

* product listings
* archives
* catalogs
* social media pages

The Source Purity Layer attempts to identify:

```text
Real opportunity
```

versus:

```text
Information adjacent to opportunity
```

---

### Importance

This layer dramatically improved recommendation quality.

Many poor recommendations disappeared after its introduction.

---

# Truth Alignment Layer

### Purpose

Anchor recommendations to reality.

The project repeatedly encountered situations where:

The ranking looked good.

Reality disagreed.

Truth Alignment attempts to reward evidence.

Examples:

* verified organizations
* confirmed opportunities
* observable signals

The layer reduces speculative recommendations.

---

### Philosophy

Truth Alignment represents a major philosophical shift.

The project became increasingly skeptical of pure scoring.

Evidence became more important than prediction.

---

# Verification Layer

### Purpose

Confirm reality.

The Verification Layer emerged from a simple realization:

A recommendation is worthless if it cannot be acted upon.

Verification attempts to answer:

* Does this organization exist?
* Is the website functional?
* Is contact information available?
* Are submissions open?
* Are deadlines visible?

---

### Current Status

Partially functional.

Current capabilities:

* homepage verification
* HTTP validation
* some contact extraction

Current weaknesses:

* submission discovery
* requirement discovery
* deadline discovery

This remains one of the highest-priority systems.

---

# Reporting Layer

### Purpose

Communicate findings.

Reports became one of the dominant outputs of the system.

Examples:

* rankings
* summaries
* dashboards
* recommendations

---

### Problem

Report generation became extremely easy.

Eventually the project accumulated:

* duplicate reports
* overlapping reports
* obsolete reports

The Reporting Layer requires consolidation.

---

# Career Layer

### Purpose

Transform opportunities into strategy.

This layer is still immature.

Its objective is to answer:

```text
What should the artist do next?
```

rather than:

```text
What opportunities exist?
```

This is one of the most important future directions.

---

# CRM Layer

### Purpose

Remember relationships.

Future examples:

```text
Gallery contacted
Date contacted
Response received
Follow-up date
Current status
```

Many artistic careers depend on relationships.

The CRM layer exists to support that reality.

---

# Quest Layer

### Future Concept

One of the most ambitious future ideas.

The observation:

Artists often know what they should do.

The difficulty is execution.

Large goals are overwhelming.

Example:

```text
Apply to twenty galleries.
```

This is psychologically difficult.

The Quest Layer breaks large goals into manageable actions.

Examples:

```text
Send one email today.
Research one gallery today.
Prepare one submission image today.
```

The purpose is not gamification.

The purpose is reducing friction.

---

# Opportunity Intelligence Layer

### Future Direction

Eventually the system should become capable of reasoning about opportunities.

Not merely finding them.

Examples:

```text
This gallery fits aesthetically but not professionally.

This contest is prestigious but unlikely.

This publication is realistic and high-value.

This opportunity should be ignored.
```

This layer does not yet exist in mature form.

It represents one of the major future goals.

---

# Architectural Principle

Every layer exists because a previous layer failed.

Discovery failed to prioritize.

Ranking failed to understand.

Artist DNA failed to respect medium.

Watercolor corrections failed to distinguish noise.

Source Purity failed to verify reality.

Verification failed to provide strategy.

Career systems emerged to fill the gap.

The architecture is therefore best understood as a sequence of lessons learned.
