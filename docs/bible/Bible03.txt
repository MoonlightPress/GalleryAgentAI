# MOCHI BIBLE

## Volume III — The Artist Model

### Why the Artist Model Exists

Most recommendation systems begin with opportunities.

Mochi eventually concluded that this is backwards.

The quality of a recommendation depends less on understanding the opportunity and more on understanding the artist.

A weak understanding of the opportunity can sometimes be corrected.

A weak understanding of the artist poisons the entire pipeline.

This realization led to the Artist Model becoming one of the foundational components of the project.

The project gradually adopted the principle:

The artist is the primary source of truth.

Not the opportunity.

Not the gallery.

Not the competition.

The artist.

---

### The Difference Between Medium and Identity

One of the earliest mistakes was treating artistic medium as identity.

For example:

```text
Watercolor
```

is not an identity.

Thousands of artists work in watercolor.

Their careers may have almost nothing in common.

Similarly:

```text
Oil Painter
Photographer
Illustrator
```

describe tools.

Not people.

The project gradually learned to separate:

Medium

from

Identity.

This became one of the most important conceptual shifts in the system.

---

### The Original Test Artist

The original test case was a watercolor painter.

However, the important characteristics were not:

```text
Watercolor
```

The important characteristics were:

```text
Urban environments
Architecture
Memory
Atmosphere
Absence
Observation
Quiet emotion
```

Those characteristics repeatedly appeared throughout the work.

They formed a recognizable visual personality.

The project became increasingly interested in identifying these deeper patterns.

---

### Atmosphere As Data

One unusual aspect of the project is that it attempts to model atmosphere.

Most recommendation systems focus on measurable facts:

* medium
* dimensions
* subject matter
* geography

Mochi attempts to model something more abstract.

Examples:

```text
Loneliness
Stillness
Memory
Distance
Quietness
Presence
Absence
```

These are difficult concepts.

Yet they strongly influence artistic fit.

Two opportunities may appear identical on paper.

One may resonate deeply with an artist.

The other may feel entirely wrong.

Atmosphere often explains that difference.

---

### Architecture

Architecture became an unexpectedly important signal.

The original artist repeatedly engaged with:

* buildings
* streets
* urban geometry
* constructed spaces

The project gradually recognized architecture as more than subject matter.

Architecture functioned as emotional context.

Buildings became memory containers.

Streets became narratives.

Spaces became emotional objects.

This realization influenced later recommendation strategies.

---

### Memory

Memory emerged as one of the strongest recurring themes.

The project eventually treated memory as a first-class signal.

Examples:

* nostalgia
* traces of human presence
* vanished people
* emotional residue
* remembered places

Many opportunities initially appeared unrelated until viewed through this lens.

The memory signal often produced better matches than medium alone.

---

### Absence

Absence became another recurring theme.

Many images communicated through what was missing rather than what was present.

Empty streets.

Quiet spaces.

Signs of life without visible people.

The project eventually recognized absence as a meaningful artistic characteristic.

This is not something traditional recommendation systems model.

It became part of the DNA approach.

---

### The Problem With Categories

Traditional art systems rely heavily on categories.

Examples:

```text
Landscape
Portrait
Urban
Watercolor
Contemporary
```

These categories are useful.

They are also crude.

The project repeatedly encountered situations where:

Two artists shared categories.

Yet had almost nothing in common.

Or:

Two artists appeared unrelated.

Yet clearly belonged in the same artistic conversation.

This observation pushed the system toward deeper aesthetic modeling.

---

### Artist DNA

Artist DNA became the project's attempt to represent artistic identity.

The goal was not perfect classification.

The goal was useful approximation.

The system attempted to answer:

```text
What kind of artist is this?
```

rather than:

```text
What medium do they use?
```

Outputs included:

* medium
* themes
* recurring motifs
* visual tendencies
* emotional tendencies
* aesthetic affinities

This became one of the strongest subsystems in Mochi.

---

### Visual Similarity Is Dangerous

The project learned that visual similarity alone can be misleading.

This lesson emerged directly from the photography problem.

Example:

A watercolor painting of an empty urban street may resemble a photograph.

Visually:

They share:

* composition
* atmosphere
* subject matter

Professionally:

They belong to different worlds.

Different publications.

Different galleries.

Different competitions.

Different communities.

This became one of the central lessons of the project.

Similarity is not identity.

---

### Professional Identity

Eventually the project became interested in a different question.

Not:

```text
What does this artist make?
```

But:

```text
Where does this artist belong?
```

This introduced professional identity.

Examples:

* gallery ecosystems
* publication ecosystems
* residency ecosystems
* collector ecosystems

The same artwork may exist inside multiple professional worlds.

Understanding those worlds became increasingly important.

---

### The Artist As A Dynamic System

The project does not assume artists remain static.

An artist evolves.

Themes evolve.

Mediums evolve.

Goals evolve.

Communities evolve.

The artist model must therefore be revisable.

The goal is not to freeze identity.

The goal is to understand identity well enough to provide useful guidance.

---

### Long-Term Goal

The ultimate purpose of the Artist Model is not classification.

It is recommendation quality.

Every component should support a larger question:

```text
What opportunities genuinely fit this artist?
```

And eventually:

```text
What should this artist do next?
```

The Artist Model exists to make that answer more accurate.
