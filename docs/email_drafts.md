# Outreach Drafts for Portfolio Artist

---

## 1. GALLERY INQUIRY

### Use case
Initial contact with galleries like KAYOKOYUKI or Thinkspace Projects when they accept unsolicited submissions or after confirming they review new artists.

### Subject line
`Portfolio submission: Contemporary realist painting`

### Email body
```
Hello,

My name is [Your Name], and I'm a painter working primarily in [watercolor/gouache/confirm your medium]. My work focuses on everyday architecture and domestic spaces in East Asia, exploring light, memory, and disappearing vernacular environments.

I've been following [Gallery Name]'s program, particularly [specific recent exhibition or artist that resonated with you]. I believe my work aligns with your interest in [their actual focus—check their website].

I'd appreciate the opportunity to share my portfolio for your consideration. I've attached a PDF with 10 recent works and a brief CV.

Thank you for your time.

Best regards,
[Your Name]
[Website]
[Instagram handle]
[Location]
```

### What to attach
- PDF portfolio: 10 images maximum, one per page, with title/medium/dimensions/year under each image
- Brief CV (1 page—education, exhibitions if any, awards if any; if your CV is thin, skip it for now)

### What to customize before sending
- Replace `[watercolor/gouache/confirm your medium]` with your actual confirmed medium
- Research the gallery: find ONE specific exhibition from the past year that genuinely connects to your work—mention it by name
- Confirm their submission process (website/Instagram)—some galleries explicitly say "no unsolicited submissions"
- Only send if you can attend an opening or ship work to that location

## 2. ART FAIR GALLERY INQUIRY

### Use case
Contacting galleries exhibiting at Affordable Art Fair Hong Kong (or similar) after identifying them as showing similar work in your price range.

### Subject line
`Inquiry: Artist representation for AAF Hong Kong`

### Email body
```
Hello,

I'm a contemporary realist painter based in [location], and I noticed [Gallery Name] exhibited at Affordable Art Fair Hong Kong in [year]. I'm reaching out because my work shares aesthetic territory with [specific artist from their roster or style they show].

My paintings focus on Asian urban architecture and domestic interiors, working in [medium]. They typically range from [size] and are priced between [your actual range based on medium—$2,500-8,000 for originals or $400-1,200 for digital].

I've attached a selection of recent work. If you're considering new artists for upcoming fairs or gallery representation, I'd welcome the chance to discuss further.

Thank you for considering my work.

[Your Name]
[Website]
[Instagram]
```

### What to attach
- PDF with 8 images that best match that gallery's aesthetic
- Only include CV if it's strong; otherwise skip it

### What to customize before sending
- Verify they actually exhibited at the fair (check fair's past exhibitor lists)
- Look at their online roster and mention one artist whose work tonally relates to yours
- Confirm your price range matches theirs (don't pitch $8,000 originals to a gallery selling $200-2,000 work)
- Only contact 3-5 galleries maximum; mass emails to all AAF exhibitors will get ignored

## 3. PRINT PUBLISHER INQUIRY

### Use case
Pitching art book publishers like PIE International or print-on-demand collaborations after you have 30+ strong images.

### Subject line
`Book proposal: Contemporary Asian architectural painting`

### Email body
```
Hello,

I'm writing to propose an art book focused on disappearing vernacular architecture in [region/country]. My work documents domestic courtyards, alleyways, and interiors through [watercolor/gouache painting], emphasizing the interplay of light and everyday material culture.

I have a completed series of [40-60] paintings exploring [specific architectural typology: e.g., "Beijing hutong courtyards" or "Hong Kong tong lau interiors"]. The work appeals to audiences interested in Asian urbanism, architectural preservation, and contemporary realism.

Comparable titles include [research 2-3 actual art books from their catalog that relate to your subject matter].

I've attached a project overview with sample images. I'd be happy to provide a full proposal if there's interest.

Thank you,

[Your Name]
[Contact info]
```

### What to attach
- PDF "book proposal": 1 page of text describing the concept + 12-15 representative images arranged attractively
- Do NOT send 60 images in one PDF; curate heavily

### What to customize before sending
- Research their catalog thoroughly—mention 2-3 actual books they've published that relate to your work
- Only send when you genuinely have 40+ cohesive images; don't pitch a concept you haven't created yet
- Specify if images are available for licensing/reproduction (confirm you own full rights)
- Be honest about your audience size if they ask (small is fine for first-time authors)

## 4. CULTURAL CENTER / COMMUNITY EXHIBITION INQUIRY

### Use case
Approaching smaller spaces, community centers, Asian cultural organizations, or libraries that host rotating exhibitions—useful for building early CV.

### Subject line
`Exhibition proposal: [Specific theme related to their mission]`

### Email body
```
Hello,

My name is [Your Name], and I create paintings documenting everyday East Asian architecture and domestic life. I'm reaching out because I believe my work aligns with [Cultural Center Name]'s mission around [their actual focus—check their website].

I'd like to propose a solo exhibition exploring [choose one focused theme: "courtyards and thresholds," "light and domestic space," "disappearing alleyways"]. The work consists of [10-20] pieces in [medium], sized [typical dimensions].

The exhibition could include [optional: artist talk, opening reception, educational component about architectural preservation].

I've attached images and a brief project description. I'm flexible on timing and happy to discuss how this might fit your programming.

Thank you for considering this.

Best,
[Your Name]
[Contact]
```

### What to attach
- PDF with 10 images + 1-page "exhibition proposal" (theme, number of works, space requirements, your 3-sentence bio)

### What to customize before sending
- Research what exhibitions they've hosted (check their website/social media for past shows)
- Propose something that genuinely fits their mission (e.g., Asian cultural centers, architecture libraries, community arts spaces)
- Be realistic: these venues often have 6-12 month booking lead times
- Mention if you can handle logistics (framing, transport, insurance, reception costs)
- This is CV-building, not income; don't expect sales or fees

## 5. COLLABORATION INQUIRY

### Use case
Reaching out to photographers, writers, architects, or other artists for potential joint projects—ONLY after you have some exhibition history or credible portfolio presence.

### Subject line
`Collaboration idea: [Specific concept]`

### Email body
```
Hello [Name],

I'm a painter whose work focuses on [your specific subject], and I've been following your [photography/writing/architectural documentation] on [where you found their work]. 

I'm particularly drawn to [specific project or series of theirs]. There's an interesting overlap between your [their approach] and my interest in [your shared subject matter].

I wanted to reach out to explore whether there might be potential for collaboration—perhaps [specific idea: joint exhibition, published dialogue, parallel documentation project]. I'm imagining something around [one-sentence concept].

No pressure or expectations—just wanted to introduce my work and see if this resonates. I've attached a few images.

Best,
[Your Name]
[Portfolio link]
```

### What to attach
- PDF with 6 images maximum, carefully chosen to show thematic overlap with their work

### What to customize before sending
- Only contact people whose work you genuinely know and respect—don't mass-email strangers
- Be specific about what you admire in their work (mention actual projects by name)
- Propose a realistic collaboration (joint exhibition requires a venue; a published conversation requires a platform)
- Keep it exploratory, not transactional
- Wait to send this until you have at least: active Saatchi presence, some online sales/favorites, or one exhibition credit
- Do NOT send this if you're at zero career visibility—build something to collaborate FROM first

## GENERAL SENDING ADVICE

**Before you send ANY of these:**

1. **Confirm your medium**—every email sounds vague without it
2. **Have an online presence**—website or active Instagram; galleries will check immediately
3. **Triple-check spelling and gallery details**—one typo kills credibility
4. **Send Tuesday-Thursday mornings**—avoid Mondays and Fridays
5. **Follow up once after 3 weeks if no response**—then move on
6. **Track who you contact**—keep a simple spreadsheet with dates/responses

**What will get you ignored:**
- Generic mass emails ("Dear Gallery")
- Overselling yourself ("highly sought-after emerging artist")
- Attaching 30 images in one email
- No website/Instagram link to verify you're real
- Sending to galleries that explicitly say "no submissions"

**What will get you considered:**
- Brevity and specificity
- Evidence you researched them (mention actual exhibitions)
- Professional images and clear information
- Realistic positioning (don't claim museum shows you haven't had)
- Following their actual submission process

**Start with email #1 (Gallery Inquiry) to KAYOKOYUKI after you:**
- Confirm your medium
- Upload 15 images to Saatchi Art
- Verify they accept submissions
- Have 10 strong images and basic CV ready

Do not send all five emails in one week. Pick ONE target, send ONE email, wait for response or 3 weeks. Slow and strategic beats mass outreach.
