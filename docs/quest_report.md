# Daily Quests
Give 5 tiny actions, each 5-15 minutes.

**1. Confirm your medium in writing**
Write one sentence: "My work is created using [watercolor/gouache/digital painting]." This single fact changes all pricing, positioning, and gallery strategy. Until you confirm this, every other action is guessing.

**2. Price check one comparable artist**
Find one artist from the dossier comparables (He Duoling, Kazuhiro Hori, Sungseok Ahn). Look at their online gallery or Artsy page. Note one price for a similar-sized work. You're building a mental map of your market tier, not copying prices.

**3. Save one architecture documentation photo**
Take or save one photo of a doorway, courtyard, or lit corner you pass today. Not for a painting—just to notice what you already notice. This feeds the observational practice that makes the work honest.

**4. Read one current artist statement from your genre**
Search "contemporary realism artist statement" and read one. Notice what language feels true and what feels like performance. You'll need to write yours soon; this is research, not imitation.

**5. Check one gallery's current exhibition online**
Visit the website of KAYOKOYUKI, Thinkspace, or TOLOT. Look at what's showing now. One image, one artist name. You're learning the context you'd enter, not stalking your competition.

# Weekly Quests
Give 5 medium actions, each 30-90 minutes.

**1. Research print-on-demand vs. giclée editions (60 min)**
Your work reproduces perfectly—this matters. Compare Saatchi Art's print-on-demand terms, INPRNT's model, and one local giclée printer's pricing. Make a simple chart: cost per print, who ships, who owns the file. This is the difference between $200 passive income and $2,000 active income per image annually.

**2. Audit your 15 strongest images (45 min)**
Go through your portfolio. Mark 15 images you'd feel honest showing a gallery today. Not "best" —strongest in the quiet, observational quality the dossier identified. Write one sentence per image about why it works. This becomes your Saatchi Art or Artsy upload set.

**3. Map your city's small gallery scene (75 min)**
If you're near an Asian city: walk or search online for 5 small galleries showing any kind of contemporary realism or illustration-adjacent work. Note names, neighborhoods, whether they show local or international artists. You're not submitting yet—you're learning your local ecosystem. If you're not near a city, research online regional galleries in the action plan cities (Tokyo, Seoul, Taipei, Hong Kong, Singapore).

**4. Study one Affordable Art Fair catalog (60 min)**
Download a past Hong Kong or Singapore AAF catalog PDF (usually free online). Look at 10 galleries' booths. Notice: price ranges, how work is photographed, booth design, artist bios. This is the commercial context where your work actually fits right now—not museums, not auctions, but here. Let that be clarifying, not discouraging.

**5. Draft a 100-word artist statement (90 min)**
Write one paragraph about what you're observing and why you paint it. Avoid: "exploring themes," "journey," "inviting the viewer." Try: "I paint [specific subject] because [honest reason]." The collaboration report notes your work's sincerity—your statement should match that tone. Save this draft; you'll revise it, but starting matters.

# Major Quests
Give 3 larger career-building actions.

**1. Set up on ONE online platform (Saatchi Art OR Artsy Emerging)**
Create account, upload 15 images with the audit from your weekly quest, write accurate descriptions, price work at $2,500-8,000 for originals if traditional media (or $400-1,200 if digital prints). Add $200-600 print options if the platform allows. This is not "selling out"—it's market testing with zero gatekeepers. You learn what people respond to, which images get favorited, whether your pricing makes sense. Give it 90 days of real presence before judging results.

**2. Produce one museum-quality print edition (if work is traditional media)**
Choose your two most successful images from platform analytics or personal conviction. Work with one giclée printer to produce 25-50 prints each, archival paper, signed and numbered. Price at $200-400. This creates an accessible entry point for your work and tests whether people will pay for your imagery at all. If work is digital, clarify your edition structure: open, limited, or one-of-one pricing.

**3. Write a gallery submission package (don't send it yet)**
Compile: 10 images (JPG, 300dpi, titled and dated), your 100-word statement, a 150-word bio (education, exhibitions if any, where you're based), and one paragraph on why you're interested in [specific gallery]. Save this as a template. The action plan identifies KAYOKOYUKI as your strongest first target—prepare the package for them specifically, but don't send until you've completed the medium confirmation and pricing research. Preparation removes the friction when the moment is right.

# Reflection Quests
Give 5 prompts that help improve the artist profile.

**1. What do you want your work to preserve?**
The dossier calls your work "elegiac"—a lament for something passing. What specifically are you mourning or documenting? Architectural styles? A pace of life? Your own memory? If you can name this precisely, your work gains conceptual clarity without losing its quietness.

**2. How do you feel about the word "decorative"?**
The analysis notes your work has "high decorative appeal" but risks being dismissed as "too decorative for serious galleries." Do you see decoration as a virtue (beauty matters, homes need art) or a limitation (not conceptual enough)? Your answer determines which galleries to approach and how to talk about the work.

**3. What's one formal risk you're afraid to take?**
The dossier notes "limited innovation in formal/technical approach." What would experimenting look like for you? Bigger scale? A single garish color? Painting at night instead of afternoon? You don't have to do it—but knowing what you're avoiding tells you whether you're being consistent or stuck.

**4. Who is this work for?**
The market analysis says: diaspora audiences, design-conscious collectors, hospitality buyers. Does that feel true? Would you rather sell 100 prints to strangers online or 5 originals to people you meet? Your answer changes whether you focus on platforms like Saatchi or pursue local gallery relationships.

**5. What would "selling out" look like to you?**
If someone commissioned 20 paintings of generic Asian alleys for a hotel chain at $5,000 each, would you do it? Where's your line between sustainable practice and integrity? The dossier says your work is "accessible but not purely commercial"—you get to define what that means.

# System Improvement Requests
Give 5 things the artist could provide to improve future recommendations.

**1. Confirm your medium and process**
State clearly: watercolor on paper, gouache, digital painting, mixed media? This changes every pricing, gallery, and production recommendation. Include: original size range, whether you work from photos or observation, how long a piece takes.

**2. Specify your location and mobility**
Are you in Beijing, Portland, Singapore, or a smaller city? Can you travel for exhibitions, or do you need local opportunities only? Physical location determines which galleries are realistic and whether shipping costs kill online sales.

**3. Describe your current audience (if any)**
Do you have: Instagram followers (how many?), past sales (volume and price?), email list, existing collectors, friend/family support, or zero current audience? This tells us whether you're building from scratch or amplifying existing traction.

**4. Name your financial goal and timeline**
Do you need: supplemental income now, full-time sustainability in 5 years, or just exhibition validation with no income pressure? Are you able to invest $500-2,000 in printing/submissions, or is everything bootstrap? Different goals require different strategies.

**5. Share your exhibition history and formal training (if any)**
Have you: shown in group shows, had a solo exhibition, attended art school, or are you fully self-taught with no exhibition CV? This determines whether you approach emerging galleries, open calls, or start with online platforms only. No judgment on any answer—just need to know where you actually are.
