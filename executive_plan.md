# Executive Plan

## Summary
You have complete strategic clarity but zero market contact. The single blocking issue is medium confirmation—every pricing, platform, and gallery decision forks on whether you make watercolor originals or digital paintings. Once confirmed, your critical path is: (1) upload to Saatchi Art for immediate market testing, (2) produce print editions to fund operations, (3) submit to KAYOKOYUKI Tokyo as your best gallery fit. Everything else—collaboration research, city analysis, group show concepts—is interesting context that will distract you from the three actions that actually test whether anyone will pay for your work.

## Do Now
- **Write one sentence confirming your medium: 'My work is created using [watercolor/gouache/digital painting/mixed media].' Send it to yourself. This unlocks everything.** (2 minutes)
  - Why: Every single recommendation—pricing ($2,500-8,000 vs $400-1,200), platform strategy (Saatchi originals vs print-on-demand), gallery targets (traditional media spaces vs digital-friendly), edition structure—depends on this fact. You're using analysis as procrastination. Stop researching and confirm what you already know.
  - Done when: You can answer 'What medium do you work in?' without hedging.
- **Create Saatchi Art account and upload 5 images today. Not 15. Not perfect descriptions. Just 5 images with accurate titles and your best-guess pricing based on medium.** (45 minutes)
  - Why: This is the only zero-gatekeeping market test available. In 30 days you'll know: which images get favorited, whether your pricing is laughable or reasonable, if anyone contacts you. All your strategic questions get answered by real collector behavior, not more analysis. Perfection is procrastination.
  - Done when: Your work is publicly viewable and priced on a platform where strangers can buy it.
- **Email one local giclée printer: 'I'm a painter interested in producing museum-quality prints. Can you send pricing for 25 prints at [specify your typical painting size]?' That's the whole email.** (10 minutes)
  - Why: Print editions are your path to sustainable income while building gallery credibility. You need one real quote to know if this costs $300 or $3,000. Knowing the number changes whether you do this next month or next year. Stop wondering, start asking.
  - Done when: You have a concrete price quote in your inbox.

## Do This Week
- **Add 10 more images to Saatchi Art. Write one-sentence descriptions: '[Subject] painted during [season/time]. Available as [original/print].' Don't agonize over poetry.** (90 minutes)
  - Why: 15 images is the minimum viable catalog. You need enough work uploaded that analytics tell you which subjects/compositions resonate. Right now you're guessing what collectors want. In two weeks you'll have data. More images = faster learning.
  - Done when: You have 15 works uploaded with prices, dimensions, and basic descriptions.
- **Research KAYOKOYUKI's current exhibition and last 10 artists shown. Make a list: Do they show more watercolor or digital? What price range? Any non-Japanese artists recently? Screenshot 3 relevant examples.** (45 minutes)
  - Why: They're your strongest gallery match, but you don't know if they're accepting submissions, if they show international artists, or if your aesthetic still fits their current direction. 45 minutes of research prevents wasting a submission or missing an obvious opportunity. This isn't analysis paralysis—it's reconnaissance before engagement.
  - Done when: You can answer: Is KAYOKOYUKI a realistic target right now, or should you wait 6-12 months?
- **Choose your 2 strongest images and get them professionally photographed or scanned at highest resolution. Title them, sign them digitally, save as 'EditionMaster_[Title]_300dpi.tif'. Do nothing else with them yet.** (2 hours (if you do scanning yourself) or $100-200 (if you hire it))
  - Why: You can't produce print editions without print-ready files. This is the prerequisite for the entire accessible editions strategy. Once you have the quote from the printer and these files ready, you're one week from having product to sell. Preparation isn't procrastination when it's the blocking technical step.
  - Done when: You have 2 print-ready master files that a printer could use tomorrow.

## Blocked Until We Know
- **What medium you actually work in: traditional watercolor/gouache originals, digital painting, or mixed/hybrid process**
  - Why it blocks progress: Pricing strategy splits here: $2,500-8,000 for traditional media originals vs $400-1,200 for digital. Platform strategy splits: Saatchi originals vs print-on-demand primary. Gallery targets split: KAYOKOYUKI/traditional spaces vs Thinkspace/digital-friendly. Edition strategy splits: precious giclée vs open digital prints. You've had complete strategic frameworks for two analysis cycles—the vagueness is a choice, not a mystery. Confirm it in 60 seconds or admit you're avoiding market contact.
- **Whether you have any existing audience: Instagram followers, past sales, email list, collector interest, exhibition history**
  - Why it blocks progress: Strategy for 0 audience (start with platforms, build proof) is different from strategy for 1,000+ followers (leverage audience for gallery interest). If you've sold work before, that changes pricing confidence. If you have a following, that's negotiating leverage. If you're truly starting from zero, that's fine—but the action sequence changes. You can answer this in one sentence but haven't.
- **Your location and mobility: Are you in Asia, North America, Europe? Can you travel for exhibitions or only pursue local opportunities?**
  - Why it blocks progress: KAYOKOYUKI is perfect if you're in Tokyo or can travel for openings—irrelevant if you're in Iowa with no travel budget. Portland galleries matter if you're US-based—useless if you're in Chengdu. Taipei is your strongest market if you're in Asia—inaccessible if you're elsewhere. Shipping costs kill online print sales from some regions. You're getting generic recommendations because you haven't provided specific constraints.

## Ignore For Now
- **All city research beyond where you actually are or can realistically show in the next 12 months**
  - Reason: Taipei, Portland, and Chengdu analysis is strategically interesting but tactically useless if you're not in those cities and can't travel. You don't need to know the 'most promising city'—you need to work with the city you're actually in. Geographic strategy is a luxury for after you have gallery representation and leverage.
- **Group show concepts ('Afternoon Light,' 'Last Alley,' 'Inhabited Objects')**
  - Reason: These are brilliant curatorial frames, but you can't pitch them without: (1) curator relationships, (2) exhibition history making you credible, (3) 2-3 other artists attached to co-exhibit. They're useful vocabulary for future opportunities, but spending time refining these concepts now is productive procrastination. Curators will reframe your work anyway.
- **Comparable artist deep-dives (He Duoling, Kazuhiro Hori, Zhang Kechun, Attasit Pokpong)**
  - Reason: You know enough. They're tonal/technical peers, they prove your market exists, you understand their positioning. Going deeper—studying their statements, pricing, exhibition histories—is research theater. It feels productive but doesn't move you toward market contact. You're not writing a thesis on contemporary Asian realism; you're trying to sell paintings.
- **Reflection quests about decoration, selling out, who the work is for, and what you're preserving**
  - Reason: These are emotionally important questions, but answering them before market contact leads to theoretical purity that real collectors will contradict. Someone will buy your work for reasons you didn't intend. A hotel will want to license it. A diaspora collector will cry looking at it. Let that happen first, then reflect on what it means. Values clarification is easier with data.
- **All collaboration research until you have something to collaborate from**
  - Reason: Partnering with photographers or sound artists for joint exhibitions is a powerful strategy when you have gallery relationships, curator interest, or exhibition-making capacity. Right now you have none of those. You can't email Zhang Kechun saying 'Let's do a show together'—where, with whose support, funded how? Collaboration is the next level, not the entry point.
