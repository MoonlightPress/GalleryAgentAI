## 0. The one-paragraph shape of the market

Japan has roughly **300 commercial galleries**, each handling **15–20 artists** — about **5,000 representation slots** against an artist population the same source puts at ~100,000, i.e. **~5%**. Source: [tagboat.tokyo — アーティストとギャラリーの新しい関係](https://tagboat.tokyo/collectorinformation/newrelationship), verbatim: 「自称も含めてアーティストと呼ばれる人たちが日本に１０万人いるとして、コマーシャルギャラリーが日本で３００軒程度、各ギャラリーが１５－２０人の取り扱いをしていると考えると、所属可能なアーティスト数は５，０００人。つまり全体の５％くらいだ。」 — **ANECDOTE / dealer estimate, named source (tagboat is itself an art dealer with a commercial interest in the framing).** No official census of Japanese commercial galleries was found. 🔴

## 1. THE MONEY, PRECISELY

### 1.1 The three models, as Japanese sources actually define them

The cleanest published definition found is from a working Japanese painter, [画家ザシキコゾウ, note](https://note.com/orientarisu/n/n7b0b8ae7e3fc) — **DIRECT**, verbatim:

| Model | Japanese term | Artist pays | Gallery takes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rental gallery** | 貸画廊 / レンタルギャラリー | 「スペース利用料(１週間で10万程度〜20万代)」 | 「作品販売手数料＊作品販売価格の**30%**が相場」 |
| **Commercial / programme gallery** | 企画画廊 / コマーシャルギャラリー | nothing for the room | 「作品販売手数料(大) ＊作品販売額の**50%**が相場」 |

> **The critical thing that is routinely missed: a rental gallery usually charges rent *and* a sales commission.** It is not "pay rent, keep 100%". Two of the three Tokyo venues checked below charge both.

**"Full representation" (専属 / 所属作家) is not a separate fee model** — it is the 企画 model plus continuity, art-fair presentation and (sometimes) exclusivity. No Japanese gallery found publishes a representation contract. 🔴

### 1.2 Does the 40–50% figure hold? — YES for 企画 galleries. Verified from five independent directions.

| Source | Type | Figure | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| [画家ザシキコゾウ](https://note.com/orientarisu/n/n7b0b8ae7e3fc) | working painter, explainer | 企画画廊 **50%** 相場 · 貸画廊 **30%** 相場 | DIRECT (of the page) |
| [Tomoko Matsuoka, note](https://note.com/tomocol_art/n/nea5e082e1182) | artist, 5 yrs international shows | 「ギャラリーや百貨店の**仲介手数料は50%が**多い。」 | ANECDOTE, named |
| [Yahoo!知恵袋 q12101659437](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q12101659437) | artists answering an artist | 「割合は**45%~50%**くらいとられました」; a second answer reports a friend paying ~**30%** and calls 30% 「決して高いとは言えません」 | ANECDOTE n=2 |
| [Masaki Hagino, note](https://note.com/masakihagino_art/n/n447d2ffc9a1c) | artist based Germany/Japan, MA Burg Giebichenstein 2019 | argues **50:50** is defensible — 「50%でもいいと思うというスタンスです」 | ANECDOTE (article is paywalled beyond the excerpt) |
| [art.yrzygs.com](https://www.art.yrzygs.com/contemporary/gallery-prices-comparison/) | secondary/SEO | artist receives **20–50%** | UNVERIFIED — programmatic-SEO site, cited only as corroboration of the band |

> **Verdict: 50% is the Japanese 企画画廊 standard and the claim is sound. 30% is the standard *rental-gallery* cut, and the two numbers get conflated constantly.** Anyone quoting "40%" was not found in any source read.

⚠️ **Payment timing is not "on the day."** 「支払に関しては**翌々月払い**くらいまでは良く有るケースです」 and 「翌月、または翌々月が多いです」 — [知恵袋 q12101659437](https://detail.chiebukuro.yahoo.co.jp/qa/question_detail/q12101659437), ANECDOTE n=2. The same thread's best answer says a 企画 solo should start with a written contract covering 「手数料の割合、経費の負担割合、将来作品が売れるなどの果実が生じた場合にどうするか」.

🔴 **GAP — no Japanese gallery publishes whether commission is taken on 税込 or 本体価格.** The book-trade convention (book report §6.1) is that 掛率 applies to 本体価格; whether galleries follow it is **UNVERIFIED**. On a ¥115,500 painting the difference between the two bases is ¥5,250 at 50%. **Pin it down in writing.**

### 1.3 Who pays for what

From the same [ザシキコゾウ page](https://note.com/orientarisu/n/n7b0b8ae7e3fc) — **DIRECT**, verbatim, the only side-by-side found:

| Item | 貸画廊 (rental) | 企画画廊 (commercial) |
|---|---|---|
| Install / invigilation / de-install | 「設営作業、お客対応、撤去作業は**作家が基本行う**。」 | 「設営作業、お客対応、撤去作業は**手伝ってくれる**。」 |

> Solve *net(rental) > net(50% gallery)*:
> - **Moon vs a 50% gallery:** rental wins above **¥200,000 本体 of sales** — about **two paintings**. Below that the commission-only gallery pays her more.

### 2.4 What the practitioner sources actually say about the unpublished route

- 「**多くのギャラリーは公募のように作品を募集しているわけでもない**」 and 「**一番いい方法はまずお客さんとしてギャラリーに行くことだと思う**」 — [keijukita, Tumblr](https://www.tumblr.com/keijukita/142604348177/), first post of a series on approaching commercial galleries. **ANECDOTE, named.** The method described is: visit repeatedly as a viewer, assess the space and the gallerist, enter the gallery's community, then approach.
- 「**ほとんどの場合、メールやメッセージを送っても連絡が返ってこないことが多い**」 — and regional (non-Tokyo) galleries reply more often than Tokyo ones. Direct messaging beats passive posting. — [画家ザシキコゾウ, note, 2019-09-22 updated 2020-08-18](https://note.com/orientarisu/n/n9b4e98a13620). **ANECDOTE, named.** The same author recommends a combined approach: send exhibition DMs, request an appointment by email, bring the portfolio *and* actual works.

> ### VERDICT on the claim
> **"Tokyo galleries do not take submissions" is not literally true, and the version of it that says "the route is attendance then a letter" is supported only by named practitioners' accounts, never by a gallery.**
>
> What is verifiable: **(a)** eight Tokyo galleries publish neither an intake nor a refusal — the door is unmarked, not locked; **(b)** at least one Tokyo contemporary gallery (biscuit) publicly and freely solicits work; **(c)** the other published routes cost money; **(d)** the "visit first, then approach" method comes from artists, not galleries, and no source anywhere quantifies its success rate. 🔴

### 6.3 The 留学 / 資格外活動 question — identical to book report §7, and it applies here too

**Selling paintings through a gallery produces the same unresolved question as selling books.** The Immigration Services Agency's own wording covers 「アルバイトなど、**収入を伴う事業を運営する活動**又は報酬を受ける活動」 — operating an income-bearing business, not just wage employment ([moj.go.jp 16-8](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-8.html), DIRECT as of 2026-09-05). The 28-hour cap and the 個人事業主 wording are set out there, and the brightest published line is that **setting up a company, hiring employees, or taking premises requires a change to 経営・管理.**

**What is different about the gallery case, and what is not:**
- **Not different:** she is receiving 報酬 from sales of her own work. The same ambiguity about 「稼働時間を客観的に確認することができる」 applies — and arguably more so, since a gallery sale produces no timesheet at all.
- **Different, and worth knowing:** a separate residence status, **「芸術」**, exists precisely for 「income-bearing musical, fine art, literary and other artistic activities」, with **painters given as an example**, granted for 5 years / 3 years / 1 year / 3 months. Its documentary requirement is 「具体的な活動の内容、期間及び行おうとする活動から生じる**収入の見込額**を記載した文書」, and where there is a contract, documents proving 「活動の内容、期間、地位及び**報酬**」; renewals require tax certificates for the past year. ([moj.go.jp — 在留資格「芸術」](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/status/artist.html), **DIRECT**.)
- **The connection worth noticing, stated as fact only:** a gallery relationship is the kind of thing that generates exactly the documents that status asks for — a stated 報酬, a stated period, a projected income. **Whether that route is available to her, when, or whether it is advisable, is not a question this report can answer.**

**Free official consultation exists** and is the right place for this: [外国人在留総合インフォメーションセンター](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/consultation/center/index.html) / [FRESC](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/support/fresc/fresc01.html).

🔴 **UNVERIFIED and deliberately not guessed:** whether a Japanese gallery withholds 源泉徴収 on payments to an artist for sales of her own work; whether a gallery will contract with a 留学 resident at all; whether any gallery asks about residence status. **Nothing was found either way.**
