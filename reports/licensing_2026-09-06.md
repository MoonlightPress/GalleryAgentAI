## 0. The five findings that decide this

1. **The framing correction is the whole report.** Licensing is not "old work vs new work." It is a **rights layer that sits on top of both**. A commission fee pays for the *labour of making the picture*; a licence pays for the *right to use it* — this book, this territory, this term, these formats. They are two separate transactions that normally happen at the same moment. A commission with no licence framing is a **buyout**, and it is permanent. §1, §7.

2. **The Japanese standard rate table for a book cover is published, and she sits in its most expensive-but-one band.** The 日本イラストレーター協会 (JIA) rate card puts a 単行本/文庫 cover at **¥30,000〜 (A) to ¥90,000〜 (E)**, and defines **Dランク as 「写実的なタッチや、水彩画などの手描きのイラスト」 — literally hand-painted watercolour — at ¥70,000〜**. The same body publishes a **二次使用料 = 70%** ladder and prices **著作権譲渡 at 2–3× the primary fee**. **DIRECT.** §2.1, §7.2.

3. **Pure licensing of an existing image for a Japanese book cover has a published price too, and it is roughly the same money.** amanaimages' rights-managed card charges a publisher **¥33,000–79,200** for a cover image, 5 years, one title, at Standard tier — ×1.5 High Standard, ×2 Premium. The independent Arcangel evidence from the English-language market converges on the same band. **The right to use an existing painting on a cover is worth about as much as painting a new one to order.** §2.2, §3.

4. **Two independent authorities — Japan's Agency for Cultural Affairs and Japan's art collecting society — both structurally exclude book covers from their standard licence terms, for the same reason.** 文化庁's free model licence assumes 「改変等することなく、そのまま利用する」 (used unaltered); JASPAR's registered tariff says its rates 「トリミング・文字乗せその他の改変を伴う利用には適用されない」 and separately carves out 「書籍の表紙（表1・表4）若しくはカバー」 entirely. **A book cover always crops the image and always puts type on it.** Covers are therefore individually negotiated by design — there is no rate card to hide behind, in either direction. **DIRECT.** §2.3, §7.3.

5. **The back-catalogue hypothesis is half right, and the half that fails is the expensive half.** Painterly, no-figure, atmospheric literary-fiction covers are genuinely ascendant — but the evidence says publishers **commission** them. The route by which an *existing* painting reaches a cover is the specialist book-cover image libraries (Arcangel, Trevillion), and those are photography-dominated. §4.

## 1. The spine: commission fee ≠ licence

> **COMMISSION FEE** — payment for the **labour** of making the picture.
> **LICENCE** — payment for the **right to use** it: this product, this territory, this period, these formats, exclusive or not.

They usually occur together and are usually invoiced as one number. That is exactly why the licence half goes unnoticed and gets given away.

**What the Japanese rate cards actually bundle.** The JIA table (§2.1) is a **制作料金** — a production fee — and it carries 一次使用 (first use) inside it. Every subsequent medium is a separate charge on the 二次使用料 ladder. So the Japanese convention is *fee = labour + first licence, bundled*, with later licences unbundled and priced as a percentage. **DIRECT**, from JIA's own wording (§7.2).

**What a pure licence looks like with no labour in it.** amanaimages and JASPAR price the right alone, on an image that already exists. §2.2 and §2.3.

**The indicative decomposition — INFERRED, and deliberately loose.** JIA's D-rank cover fee is **¥70,000〜**; amanaimages' pure-licence cover band at comparable reproduction sizes is **¥39,600–46,200** (Standard, ~B6 to ~B5). Taken at face value that implies roughly **¥40,000–46,000 of "right to use" and ¥24,000–30,000 of "labour"** in a Japanese book cover. ⚠️ **These two tables price different markets to different customers and the populations are not comparable** — one is a trade association's guidance to illustrators, the other a stock agency's list price to publishers. Treat the split as a *shape*, not a number. What it does establish reliably is that **the rights half is not a rounding error; on this evidence it is the larger half.**

**The failure mode, stated plainly.** If she is paid ¥70,000 to paint a cover and the contract says 著作権譲渡 or contains no licence scope at all, then: she cannot sell that image as a print; she cannot license it to a puzzle company; she cannot put it in her own book; she may not be able to show it on her own site. See §7.4 for the documented Japanese cautionary case.

It is nonetheless the most widely circulated reference figure in the Japanese illustration market and the number an illustrator can point at.

**(2) 文庫版、新書版又はそれに準じる版型のもの — 基準料金・優遇料金ともに、上記(1)の料金の80%とする。**

**The print-run finding — INFERRED arithmetic on the table above.** A full-page colour licence rises from **¥46,000 at 3,000 copies to ¥57,000 at 100,000 copies: +23.9% for 33× the print run.** For books, circulation barely moves the price. That is a genuinely useful thing to know before negotiating: **arguing about print run on a book is arguing about ~20%.**

**第2-2 雑誌 — and the opposite finding.** Magazine rates *do* scale hard with circulation. 1頁以下 カラー 基準料金: **¥51,000** @≤15,000部 → ¥89,000 @≤100,000 → ¥171,000 @≤800,000 → **¥561,000** @≤6,000,000. A 見開き2頁 colour spread at 6M circulation is **¥911,000**. **DIRECT.** For editorial reuse, circulation is the whole negotiation.

**⚠️ THE CARVE-OUT — and it is the most important sentence in this section.** JASPAR's 第1 総則 defines category 1 (書籍への複製及び譲渡) and then excludes, verbatim:

> 「ただし、**書籍の表紙（表1・表4）若しくはカバーに複製する場合**、モノグラフィー若しくはその大部分が特定の作家の作品により構成される書籍に複製する場合、又は解題付き類別目録（カタログ・レゾネ）を作成する場合を除く。」

And 第1-2, verbatim:

> 「本規程に定める使用料は、著作物の全部を改変することなく利用する場合に適用されるものであり、**トリミング・文字乗せその他の改変を伴う利用には適用されない**。」

**Japan's registered art-licensing tariff has no price for a book cover, on two separate grounds.** Covers are excluded by name, and covers necessarily involve cropping and type. The rates above are the **floor for interior reuse**, and the cover is explicitly a negotiated object priced above them. That is a market structure fact, not a research failure.

**JASPAR membership:** 「ご入会は無料です。」 — free to join; contact them and they send an application form ([jaspar.or.jp/enrolment](https://jaspar.or.jp/enrolment), **DIRECT**). Scope is 「美術・写真・イラストレーション・漫画・舞台芸術等作家」. Commission is described only as 「事務手数料控除の上」 — 🔴 **the actual percentage is not published.** No nationality or residence requirement is stated on that page (**DIRECT, absence** — not the same as permission).

## 4. ⭐ Testing the hypothesis: is her back catalogue unusually suited to book covers?

**Hypothesis as stated:** literary fiction covers use quiet urban architecture, light and atmosphere with no figures — exactly what she has thousands of.

**Verdict: the aesthetic claim holds. The economic claim does not follow from it.**

### What supports it

- **SPINE Magazine**, the book-cover design trade publication, in its 2025 review / 2026 predictions piece ([spinemagazine.co, 2026-02-04](https://spinemagazine.co/articles/book-cover-design-trends-in-2025-predictions-for-2026), by Linnea Gradin) records covers built on **"handpainted artwork"** demonstrating a commitment to **"real brush strokes and lasting pieces of art"**, explicitly framed as a **"push back on AI book cover design."** **DIRECT quote.**
- The same piece, on subject matter: **"The motifs are also increasingly taken from the natural world, rather than focusing on Renaissance people and Victorians in distress"** — a documented movement *away* from figures. **DIRECT.**
- Arcangel — a business that exists only to sell book covers — maintains **illustration as a co-equal top-level category** alongside photography, and a dedicated book-cover-illustration page. Painted work on covers is a real, transacted market, not an aspiration. **DIRECT.**
- The JIA rate card prices **Dランク 水彩画 hand-painted work above three of its five ranks** for exactly this use. The Japanese market has a published price for hand-painted covers because it buys them. **DIRECT.**

### What undercuts it

- 🔴 **Every "2026 book cover trends" source found except SPINE was a design-agency content-marketing page** (Miblart, Damonza, drawphics, 360illustrationhouse, aibookart, bookcoverhub). These are the licensing equivalent of the programmatic-SEO logistics sites flagged in the book economics report: **DIRECT reads of published pages, but worthless as market evidence.** None is quoted in this report.
- **The trend evidence describes commissioning, not licensing.** SPINE "extensively credits specific artists and designers for commissioned paintings" and **makes no statement comparing licensing existing artwork to commissioning new work.** The rise of painterly covers is a rise in *commissions*.
- **The structural obstacle is real and is documented twice.** Both 文化庁's model licence (「改変等することなく、そのまま利用する」) and JASPAR's tariff (「トリミング・文字乗せ…を伴う利用には適用されない」) presume an unaltered image. A cover crops to a trim size with a spine and a bleed, and lands type across it. **An existing square or landscape watercolour of a Tokyo street was not composed with a title area, and that is the thing an art director is actually shopping for.**
- Arcangel's **literary fiction collection is its smallest at 10,000 images**, against 857,702 contemporary fiction. The niche the hypothesis targets is the thinnest shelf in the shop.
- 🔴 **No count of how much of Arcangel's or Trevillion's architecture/cityscape holding is painted rather than photographed could be obtained.** Arcangel's search returned 404 to WebFetch, Trevillion returned 403 throughout, and WebSearch was exhausted. **This is the single measurement that would settle the question and it was not made.**

### The honest reading

**The subject matter is right and the medium is in fashion. The transaction she is imagining — a publisher browsing a back catalogue and paying to use painting #1,847 as-is — is the rarer of the two available transactions.** The commoner one is: a book designer sees the body of work, likes the atmosphere, and **commissions a cover in that manner**. On the corrected framing (§1) that is still licensing — it is still a licence she should scope and keep — but it is new work, and it is a relationship, not a catalogue query.

**The catalogue is not wasted in that scenario.** It is the portfolio that gets her the commission, and it is a standing inventory for §6's product categories, where "already exists, unaltered, no title area needed" is a *feature* rather than a problem.

## 7. Contract shape — the rights layer in detail

### 7.1 What a licence is scoped by

Arcangel's own description of a rights-managed licence names the axes, **DIRECT**: *"RM licences custom-built around each project so you can define **usage, territory, duration and print run** with precision."* amanaimages' book card adds two more in practice: **placement** (表1 vs 表4 vs 中面 — worth ~1.54×) and **format** (print vs the separately-priced 「PDF/電子化流用（期限なし）」).

**The six axes, and what the evidence says each is worth:**

| Axis | What it controls | What the evidence says it is worth |
|---|---|---|
| **Use / medium** | This book, this edition — not "our books" | **The biggest lever in Japanese practice.** JIA's ladder charges **70%** of the original fee for the *second* medium, 50% for the third and fourth, 20% from the fifth. **DIRECT** |
| **Placement** | Front cover vs back vs interior | **~1.54×** front over back or interior, at every size (amanaimages). **DIRECT** |
| **Term** | Five years, not forever | amanaimages' standard book licence is 「使用期間 ～5年」; 図鑑 get 10. Beyond that, 「使用期間を超えての増刷等は別途料金」. **DIRECT** |
| **Territory / language** | Japan, or Japanese-language, not world | Named as a pricing input by Arcangel's calculator. 🔴 **No published multiplier found in any source.** |
| **Print run** | Copies covered | **Books: nearly free — +23.9% for 33× the run** (JASPAR). **Magazines: the whole negotiation — ×11 for ×400 circulation** (JASPAR). **DIRECT, INFERRED arithmetic** |
| **Exclusivity** | Whether she can license it elsewhere | 文化庁's model contract **defaults to non-exclusive**: 「著作権者は、利用者以外の者にも著作物の利用を許諾することや、著作権者自身が利用することができます（非独占的許諾）」. **DIRECT** |

**On worldwide / all-formats / perpetual specifically.** 🔴 **No source read this session publishes a multiplier for a global, all-media, perpetual licence.** The closest published anchor is JIA's rule that outright **著作権譲渡 costs 2–3× the primary fee** (§7.2) — an unlimited licence is the same thing in economic substance, so **2–3× is the only defensible floor to reason from, and it is an inference from an adjacent rule, not a quoted rate.**

### 7.2 ⭐ Japan: 著作権譲渡 vs 利用許諾 — the highest-value material in this report

**The JIA ladder, verbatim** ([jpn-illust.com/guarantee.html](https://jpn-illust.com/guarantee.html), **DIRECT**):

> 「日本イラストレーター協会では追加媒体料を次のように定めています。
> 二次使用料　7割
> 三次使用料　5割
> 四次使用料　5割
> 五次使用料〜　2割」

**And the buyout premium, verbatim, from the same page — read this one twice:**

> 「著作権譲渡の条件で2倍の料金10,000円もらったとしても、**後々大損することにもなりかねません**。通常、日本イラストレーター協会では著作権譲渡の場合は、**一次使用の2倍から3倍程度**の料金設定にしていますが、**その後に使用する可能性のある媒体をできる限り聞き出してから**決めるようにしています。」

**Applied to her band (INFERRED, arithmetic on JIA's own D-rank 単行本/文庫 figure of ¥70,000):**

| Deal | What she gets | What she gives up |
|---|---|---|
| One cover, one medium (一次使用) | **¥70,000** | Nothing else |
| + a second medium (e.g. 電子書籍) | +70% = **¥49,000** | Nothing else |
| + a third (e.g. 広告) | +50% = **¥35,000** | Nothing else |
| **著作権譲渡 (buyout)** | **¥140,000–210,000** | **Everything, forever** — prints, products, her own book, arguably her own website |

**Two mediums licensed separately (¥119,000) already beat the low end of a full buyout.** Three (¥154,000) beat it comfortably. **That is the arithmetic that makes the case.**

**How common is 著作権譲渡 in Japanese illustration?** From **MARU**, a working Tokyo freelance illustrator (b.1996, 女子美術大学, credits including Zexy, JJ, non-no, with) writing at [ksd-illust.com/nijisiyouryou](https://ksd-illust.com/nijisiyouryou) — **ANECDOTE, n=1, named and credentialed:**

- Companies **increasingly request buyouts specifically to avoid repeated per-medium fee negotiations** across platforms.
- On what transfer means: **「権利を譲渡するということは、制作したイラストが自分のものではなくなることを意味します」** — verbatim.
- Consequences she names: cannot show it on her own site, cannot control modification or commercialisation.
- Her own practice: she negotiates **30–50%** of the production fee for secondary use — i.e. **below JIA's 70% guidance.** Worth knowing that the published number is an aspiration, not a floor.

A second working Japanese designer/illustrator, **takahito** ([takahitoart.com/design-niji-cost](https://takahitoart.com/design-niji-cost/), **ANECDOTE**), prices 二次使用 at **20–80% of the production fee depending on medium reach** — 「より影響がある媒体に使われるほど金額は高くなります」 — with worked examples on a ¥10,000 base: website 80%, flyer 50%, LINE icon 20%. For 著作権譲渡 he reports being asked for **2× to 10×**, or **¥20,000–100,000 additional**, and notes large corporations may be charged **¥300,000+**.

### 7.3 ⭐ The Japanese government publishes a free model licence — and pointedly does not publish a transfer one

文化庁 (Agency for Cultural Affairs) runs the **著作権契約書作成支援システム**, free, at [pf.bunka.go.jp/chosaku/chosakuken/c-template/](https://pf.bunka.go.jp/chosaku/chosakuken/c-template/). **DIRECT.**

Eleven templates. Two are directly hers:

- **#4 イラストの作成（ポスター・パンフレットなどの作成）** — commissioned new illustration
- **#9 既存の原稿やイラスト、写真、自作の楽曲・映画、舞踊・無言劇などの利用許諾** — **licensing existing work**

**And the omission is the finding.** The system states, verbatim: **「著作権の譲渡等、本システムでは提供していないひな形がありますので」** — *copyright assignment is among the templates this system does not provide.* **The Japanese government will help a creator write a licence for free, and declines to help them write away their copyright.**

**The default terms of template #9, verbatim (DIRECT):**

> 「対象著作物を、**改変等することなく、そのまま利用する**ことを前提」
> 「利用者は、作品を利用するにあたり、**具体的な利用態様を事前に著作権者に示し、その承諾を得る**もの」
> 「著作権者は、利用者以外の者にも著作物の利用を許諾することや、著作権者自身が利用することができます（**非独占的許諾**）」

**Non-exclusive by default; use disclosed in advance and approved; image unaltered.** That is the shape of a licence the Japanese state considers normal. Also available: [誰でもできる著作権契約マニュアル](https://pf.bunka.go.jp/chosaku/chosakuken/keiyaku_intro/chosakukenkeiyaku_manual.pdf) (⚠️ 4.4MB, image-only PDF — **could not be text-extracted this session; its contents are UNVERIFIED**).

⚠️ **Note the collision with §4.** Template #9 presumes an unaltered image. A book cover crops and overlays type. **The government's free licence template does not, as written, cover a book cover** — the same structural gap JASPAR's tariff has.

### 7.4 The one clause that costs an artist years

**A buyout is not a bigger fee. It is the end of the asset.**

The canonical Japanese cautionary case, raised by MARU and **independently verified** against [ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/およげ!たいやきくん](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/およげ!たいやきくん) — **DIRECT:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Performer | 子門真人 |
| Payment | **「子門は5万円」 — ¥50,000, a one-off session fee, no royalty** |
| B-side performer なぎらけんいち | 「なぎらは3万円」 — ¥30,000 |
| Sales | **「オリコン調べで450万枚以上」 — over 4.5 million copies** |
| Ex-gratia afterwards | 「後にヒット記念としてレコード会社から100万円と白いギターが1本贈られた」 — ¥1,000,000 and a guitar, as a gift |

⚠️ **It is a music case, not illustration.** It is cited because it is the example Japanese creative-industry writing actually uses, and because the mechanism is identical: a flat fee accepted in place of a licence, on a work that then earned for decades.

**The specific clause to fear, in her situation, is not a single cover deal.** It is **an unlimited buyout across a body of work** — a contract that acquires "the Works" or "all illustrations delivered under this agreement" with no medium, term or territory limit. With six years of daily painting behind her, a single sentence of that shape can take thousands of images out of her control permanently, and there is no mechanism to get them back.

### 7.5 Rights managed vs royalty free — what it means for her

| | **Rights Managed (RM)** | **Royalty Free (RF)** |
|---|---|---|
| What the buyer gets | A licence "custom-built around each project so you can define usage, territory, duration and print run with precision" | "a more flexible usage framework within a single fee" |
| Price | Per use, scaled by the six axes in §7.1 | One fee, broad use |
| Repeat income | **Yes** — every new use is a new fee | **No** — one payment, then unlimited use within the framework |
| Arcangel's mix | **"the majority of their collection"** is RM | minority |
| Restriction on her | RM contributors "will not distribute similar content (from the same shoot, production or obtained otherwise)" elsewhere | RF submissions may not go to microstock |

All quotes **DIRECT** from [arcangel.com/pricing](https://arcangel.com/pricing/) and [arcangel.com/contributor-terms-and-conditions](https://arcangel.com/contributor-terms-and-conditions/).

**For an artist with a large, atmospheric, non-repeating catalogue, RM is the structurally correct model** — it is the only one where the same painting can be paid for more than once. RF is a one-time sale wearing a licence's clothes.

**NO LEGAL ADVICE. What follows is what the documents say and what they do not say.**

### 8.1 The immigration question

The Immigration Services Agency defines what 資格外活動許可 is for, verbatim ([moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-8.html](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/applications/procedures/16-8.html), **DIRECT**, re-verified 2026-09-06):

> 「許可された在留資格に応じた活動以外に、アルバイトなど、**収入を伴う事業を運営する活動又は報酬を受ける活動**を行おうとする場合」

Application is **free of charge**, standard processing 「２週間～２か月」. Criteria include 「現在の在留資格に係る活動を行っていること」 (she must actually be studying) and 「素行が良好であること」.

**The hinge, and it is genuinely unresolved.** The permission governs **activities** — 「事業を運営する活動」 and 「報酬を受ける活動」. A licence fee is legally **対価 for the use of a right**, not 報酬 for performing an activity; the painting was made in the past and no work is done when the money arrives. That reading would place passive royalty receipts outside the activity test entirely.

🔴 **Against that: no official source was found that says so.**
- **Nothing in the ISA's own material distinguishes passive/royalty income from wage or business income.** **DIRECT (verified absence on the 資格外活動 page).**
- The 2026-09-05 book economics report reached the same wall on the adjacent question and recorded the two clear edges it could find: sole-proprietor activity within 28 hrs/week is *likely* inside; **incorporating, hiring staff, or taking premises requires a change to 経営・管理** — 「新たに法人を設立する場合や従業員を雇用する場合、事業所を設けて活動する場合等は、…「経営・管理」の在留資格への変更が必要となります。」 Those edges are unchanged by anything found this session.
- 🔴 **The 28-hour cap and the 個人事業主 wording could not be re-verified this session.** The page carrying them is not `16-8.html` (checked — it is not there), two guessed ISA URLs returned 404, and WebSearch was exhausted. **They are carried over from the earlier report on its own citation, not re-confirmed here.**
- 🔴 **The hours problem is worse for royalties than for sales.** The blanket permission reaches sole-proprietor work only where 稼働時間を客観的に確認することができる. **Royalty income has no hours attached to it at all** — there is nothing to count either way. Whether that puts it safely outside the cap or awkwardly outside the framework **is not answerable from public sources.**

**Free official consultation exists and is the right place for this question:** [外国人在留総合インフォメーションセンター](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/consultation/center/index.html) and [FRESC](https://www.moj.go.jp/isa/support/fresc/fresc01.html).
