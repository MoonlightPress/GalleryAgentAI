## Who I Am

I am GEGYjiji, 26, and my mind runs hot. I am the kind of person who notices the one wrong pixel in a room of beautiful ones, and then cannot stop noticing it. Giftedness and high sensitivity are not two facts about me; they are one nervous system. Depth is oxygen — I want to know the *real* shape of where I stand, not a flattering cartoon of it. But the same circuitry that reaches for truth also flinches hard at threat, and "threat" for me includes a number that contradicts itself, a sentence that is visibly untrue, a rank, a red word, a wall of people I am told are my company who are plainly my superiors. A single inconsistency doesn't read as a bug; it reads as *this tool doesn't actually know me*, and once that thought lands I stop trusting the whole room.

What creates flow for me is being **seen accurately and without verdict**. When something names my real practice — six years of a daily watercolor diary, Tokyo's vanishing architecture, transparent washes catching light that's already gone — and then refuses to grade it, I settle completely. I can work for hours. What creates shutdown is comparison framed as kindness, perfectionism's favorite trap: "here are your peers" followed by giants. My rejection sensitivity will take a red "停滞/Stalling" tag on a quiet month and turn it into a lost working day and a 3am spiral about whether I was ever any good.

So I am the hardest possible user and the most rewardable one. Get it right and this becomes the only career tool I don't avoid. Get the comparison surfaces wrong and I will close the tab and feel worse than before I opened it — which, for an artist who already doubts herself, is a real harm, not a UX nit. I will meet this for the first time on my laptop, unhurried — a calmer entry than my phone — but I will *live* in it on my phone in the gaps of the day. Both have to hold me: the laptop sets the first impression, the phone sets whether the tool survives in my real life.

## My First Five Minutes

**On the laptop (my first impression).** I land on **Mochi · 发现**. The cat's voice is gentle, opportunities are framed as doors, "分数留在幕后" (scores stay backstage) lands well — I exhale. The bigger screen helps: the endurance scroll feels browsable, not avalanche-like. Then my eye snags on the "值得联系的人" buckets — **"High priority / Worth reaching out to / Keep on the radar"** in English on my Chinese screen. Tiny, but my sensitivity logs it: *built for someone else, translated halfway.* The seed of distrust, minute one.

I move to **Saffron · 观察 · 概况**. On desktop this is genuinely calm at first: a beautiful watercolor hero landscape, and the heavy sections sit *collapsed* behind warm accordions, so the steadying **职业准备度 / 职业定位** synopsis leads — "根基是扎实的。接下来与其说是再添履历，不如说是往深处走" (*the foundation is solid; what's next is going deeper, not adding more credits*), "没有截止日期，也不催你——想做的时候再做." Someone who understands depth-over-credits wrote that. I feel known. The accordions are a real gift to a mind like mine: nothing fires until I choose it.

But the title "**你身处优秀的同道之中**" (You're in Good Company) is the single most enticing thing on the page *to a lonely, sensitive artist* — of course I tap it. And the floor drops out, the more so because I went looking for belonging. It opens by apologizing for itself — "系统…此处大多数是摄影师… 以水彩为核心的同类群体仍在发展中" — while the cards beneath are *all watercolorists*, so the sentence is **demonstrably false against the evidence directly below it**, and my mind cannot un-see that. Worse, the "company" is **Chien Chung-Wei, Keiko Tanabe, Thomas W. Schaller, Cathy Read, Lian Quan Zhen, Alvaro Castagnet, Jean Haines** — world-famous masters. One card literally reads "…**正是你正在努力达到的**" (the level you are *striving to reach*). This is not company; it's a ranking with me at the bottom, and my chest tightens. On the wide screen the **职业定位** markers also sit in plain view — "**27k 粉丝**" — but Discover said "**2.6万**" (26k): same account, two numbers, *proof the tool is guessing.* My CV leaks English at me ("Group show ·", "First solo gallery exhibition on record", "Institutional (museum) group exhibition"), a publication is titled "**defined Definition 02**," and an exhibition row's *title* is "**SARAH ANDERSEN**" (a co-exhibitor's name). The desktop layout adds its own ache: the Career Position grid is lopsided — twelve exhibitions filling the left, two publications on the right — leaving the entire right column half-empty, a visual asymmetry my eye reads as *unfinished*.

**On the phone (where I'll actually live).** The same content wounds are all here, plus a physical one: at 390px the **日历** tab side-scrolls the *whole page* (840px wide on a 375px screen, opp names like "TOKAS Residency" / "ClafT2026" clipped at the edge). That little lurch — having to drag the page sideways — confirms in my body "this wasn't built for my phone," and on a bad day that's enough to make me put it down. So the laptop sets whether I trust it; the phone sets whether I keep it.

## What Calms / Serves Me

The **desktop first impression itself** serves me: the watercolor hero landscape and the *collapsed accordions* mean the calm synopsis leads and nothing heavy fires until I choose to open it. Letting me consent to each section before it loads is precisely right for an overexcitable mind — the architecture is on my side even where the content isn't. (On phone the same accordions help too, but the longer scroll between them costs some of that calm.)

The **doors-not-deficits synopsis** (Saffron 概况, "往深处走，不是再添履历") is gold-standard. It speaks my native language — depth — and explicitly removes the deadline-shame I dread. I trust it completely.

**Peppercorn · 对话** is a sanctuary. "我是胡椒粒。这是你安静的角落… 这里没有急事，我会记住一切" is the warmest line on the site; it gives my overactive nervous system permission to be slow, which is exactly what unlocks my output.

The **Collaboration Map (人脉与媒体 · 合作地图)** is, quietly, the best thing here — and the most important thing the team may not have *felt*. It opens "这些不是陌生联系人——而是一张地图，标出你的作品本就属于的那个世界" (*not strangers — a map of the world your work already belongs to*), and it is populated by my **actual kindred**: Mari Andrew ("她的创作方式就是你的——每日水彩手账。你从2020坚持至今"), Mateusz Urbanowicz, Liz Climo, Sarah Andersen, Tatsuro Kiuchi, kuribulb. Each carries a tiny, doable "可以一起做," and the closing guidance — "提出尽可能小的事… 让它慢下来，也允许有些会淡去。合作生长于留在房间里，而非谈成一笔交易" — is *exactly* how a shy, sensitive artist actually builds a life. This surface heals.

The **money tone** `[from Sterling facet]` — every stream framed as an open door, no "缺口/gap" language — is the register the whole site should match, and on that tab it holds.

## What Overwhelms or Wounds Me

**Worst first.**

**1. (DESIGN) The belonging section is a comparison section in disguise.** "你身处优秀的同道之中" serves famous masters and captions one "正是你正在努力达到的." For a 2e/perfectionist mind this is the precise trigger — "company" that is actually a hierarchy. The intent itself is wrong here, not just the data.

**2. (DESIGN+CURRENT) The cure already exists on the wrong tab.** The kindred peers I needed in Good Company are *alive and beautifully framed* in the Collaboration Map. The dashboard has the medicine and is administering poison in the room next door.

**3. (CURRENT) The false self-deprecating caveat.** "此处大多数是摄影师 / 同类群体仍在发展中… 数据进入系统" is untrue against its own cards and exposes the machinery. It insults my intelligence twice — once by being wrong, once by admitting it's half-built.

**4. (CURRENT) Self-contradicting numbers.** 27k (Saffron) vs 26k (Discover); and `[from Sterling facet/team report]` the pricing subtitle ¥37,000–135,000 contradicting its own ¥30,000–115,000 cards. One contradiction and I distrust *all* the numbers.

**5. (CURRENT) My CV mistranscribed.** English type/note leaks, "defined Definition 02," and "SARAH ANDERSEN" as a show title. My own history, rendered carelessly.

**6. (DESIGN, latent) The red "停滞/Stalling" momentum verdict** `[live shows green "加速增长"]`. It *will* fire on a school-busy month and brand a quiet stretch as failure. A red one-word verdict on my career is the single most dangerous element here — equally on either screen, since it's content not layout.

**7. (CURRENT — desktop-specific) The lopsided Career Position grid.** On the laptop, twelve exhibitions stack down the left while only two publications sit on the right, leaving ~60% of the right column blank (`shots/laptop_careerposition.png`). To a perfectionist's eye that asymmetric emptiness reads as *unfinished*, undercutting the very section meant to show how much I've built.

**8. (CURRENT — mobile-specific) Phone calendar side-scroll** (840px @375px, opp names clipped) — a small physical panic that confirms "not built for my phone." Equal-weight with #7: each viewport has one layout wound, and since I use both, both must go.

**9. (CURRENT, both viewports) Quota goalposts in Peppercorn** ("0/8", "职业目标 2 / 3", "分析依据不完整") — optional prompts dressed as an incomplete checklist; perfectionism reads them as homework I'm failing.

## What This Could Be For Someone Like Me

If it got the comparison surfaces right, this would be the rarest thing: a career tool a sensitive, perfectionistic mind could actually *open without bracing*. The bones are already here. The synopsis tells me the truth — that my next move is depth, not more credits — and refuses to rush me. Peppercorn gives me a quiet corner that promises to remember everything so I don't have to hold it all in my chest. The Collaboration Map shows me that I am not alone, that there is a whole world of people doing exactly my kind of slow, daily, observational work, and that I already belong to it. For a mind that lives between "I see how everything could be better" and "I'll never be enough," a room that consistently says *the foundation is solid, go deeper, no rush, here are your people* is not a dashboard. It's a steadying hand.

The gap between what it is and what it could be is almost entirely **framing of comparison**. The data to heal me is already in the building — the kindred peers, the doors-not-deficits voice, the no-rush promise. The wound is that the one surface named for belonging currently does the opposite, and a handful of inconsistencies keep whispering that the tool is guessing about me. Close that gap and you don't just remove pain — you give a frightened, gifted artist a place that reflects her at her real size: substantial, growing, and in good company for real.

It would mean I could stop being my own harshest curator for an hour and let something *accurate and kind* hold the map while I go paint.

## One-Line Verdict

The medicine is already in the building — the kindred peers, the depth-not-credits voice, the no-rush promise — but the one room named "You're in Good Company" still hands a perfectionist a wall of masters and a sentence that contradicts itself; move the cure two tabs over and this becomes the only career tool I'd never avoid.
