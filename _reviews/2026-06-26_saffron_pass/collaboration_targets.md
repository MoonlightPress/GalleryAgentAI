## How to approach a collaboration / 如何开启一次合作

*No pressure, no scoreboard. Small is the whole secret — small is what people say yes to. / 不施压，不计分。"小"就是全部的秘诀——人们答应的，正是"小"。*

**English.**
1. **Start where you already are.** Spend a while just being a real reader of their work — a genuine comment, a save, a share. Let your name become familiar before you ever ask for anything.
2. **Send one short, specific message.** Not a pitch. One honest line about a piece of theirs you love, and that you're a watercolor illustrator keeping a daily diary in Tokyo. No ask attached yet.
3. **Propose the smallest possible thing.** A print swap, one shared post, a single page in a zine. Small is easy to say yes to — and it's the seed of everything larger.
4. **Lead with your daily diary.** Six years of it is your most distinctive gift, not a request. "I keep a daily watercolor diary" opens more doors than any portfolio.
5. **Let it be slow, and let some fade.** Some threads become a show; some quietly don't, and that's completely fine. Collaboration grows from staying in the room, not from closing a deal.

**中文。**
1. **从你已经在的地方开始。** 先花一段时间，只是真心做他们作品的读者——一句真诚的评论、一次收藏、一次转发。在你开口之前，先让你的名字变得熟悉。
2. **发一条简短而具体的消息。** 不是推销。只用一句真心话，说出你喜欢他们的哪一幅，并说明你是一位在东京坚持每日手账的水彩插画家。先不要附带任何请求。
3. **提出尽可能小的事。** 一次版画互换、一条共同发布、合志里的一页。"小"才容易被答应——而它正是一切更大事情的种子。
4. **以你的每日手账开场。** 六年的坚持是你最独特的礼物，而不是一个请求。"我坚持画每日水彩手账"，比任何作品集都更能打开门。
5. **让它慢下来，也允许有些淡去。** 有些线会长成一场展览，有些会安静地无果，这都完全没关系。合作生长于"留在房间里"，而非"谈成一笔交易"。
