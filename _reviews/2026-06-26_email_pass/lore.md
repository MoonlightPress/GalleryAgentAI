## 3. BATCH-LEVEL FINDINGS (variety / sameness — criterion 5)

This is where the batch is weakest, and it is the headline.

- **The signature image-list is recycled in ~17 of 20.** "赤い壁、路地、プール、緑の池 / red walls, alleyways, pools, green ponds" (and the EN tail "the particular quality of light in a familiar street") appears, near-verbatim, in almost every draft (01, 02, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07, 08, 09, 12, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18, 20…). Used **once**, it is her authentic voice. Used across a whole batch it becomes a mail-merge token: when she reads draft #5 and meets the same sentence from draft #1, the "this sounds freshly like me" feeling collapses. This is the single biggest threat to the relief bar, and it lowers even the individually-READY drafts when read in sequence.
- **The "transforms the ordinary into something moving" line repeats too:** JA "感動的なシーンへ(と)変える" and EN "something (I hope feels) quietly moving" recur across many drafts. Same idea, same words.
- **Opener templates cluster.** EN openers collapse into two shapes — "I came across X and wanted to reach out" (01, 08, 15) and "I am writing to ask about" (03, 20). JA openers nearly all use the venue-praise clause "〜を拝見し／に親しみを感じており、ご連絡いたしました." The venue-specific detail varies (good), but the *shape* is uniform.
- **Three gallery-call JA drafts (12, 13, 14) share one skeleton:** opener → diary → image-list → Colour Diary → Tide from China → close. Read together they are visibly one template with the gallery name swapped.

Net: individually most drafts are fine; **as a set they read as generated, because the most personal sentence in each is the same sentence.**

## 4. STANDARDS — voice & language rules a draft must meet
*(written to be dropped straight into the generator's prompt)*

**A. Openers**
1. EN: never open with "I am writing to inquire/ask about…", "I am reaching out regarding…", or any throat-clear. Open with one true, specific detail about *this* venue, or a plain human line.
2. Rotate openers: no two drafts in a batch may use the same opener shape. Keep ≥3 distinct opener patterns per batch.
3. JA: the "〜を拝見し／に親しみを感じており、ご連絡いたしました" opener is good, but each must carry a *different, specific* observation — not the venue name swapped into the same clause.

**B. Keigo & register (JA)**
4. Match register to venue type: café / zine shop / playful shop (Kamome, mona records, Village Vanguard) = warm 丁寧語, さん, lighter touch; juried gallery / society / fair (biscuit, Gallery and Links81, 日本水彩展) = ご担当者様 + 申し上げます／存じます. Don't put society-level stiffness on a café, or café-casual on a society.
5. Loanwords must be correct and, where natural, Japanese-first: 委託販売（コンサインメント), never コンスメント. No English-word-order sentences carried into Japanese.

**C. Language selection**
6. Domestic Japanese institutions, societies, galleries, bookshops, cafés → Japanese. Reserve English for genuinely international/Anglophone targets (Canadian Society, Printed Matter, Offprint) or confirmed-bilingual international competitions.
7. Never embed a Japanese-script venue name in an English sentence (or vice-versa); render the name in the email's language.

**D. Sentence length (EN)**
8. No sentence over ~30–35 words; break run-ons. Her voice is quiet and observational — short clear sentences read more human than one breathless paragraph.

**E. One-statement-of-practice rule (anti-sameness — the critical one)**
9. The red-walls/alleyways/pools/green-ponds image-list may appear in **no more than ~1 in 4 drafts per batch, and never in identical wording.** For every other draft, pull a *different* true image from her world — interior light, a cat on a windowsill, rain-grey Tokyo streets, a familiar park corner, the seasons turning, curtains and shadow. The profile lists many; rotate them.
10. One practice-statement per email, freshly phrased. Vary the "ordinary → moving" line too; do not recycle "感動的なシーンに変える" / "something quietly moving" verbatim.

**F. Anti-template / variety**
11. No two drafts in a batch may be structurally identical (opener + practice line + credits block + close). Vary which credentials appear — a café needs only diary + Colour Diary; a juried gallery call warrants Tide from China and exhibition history too. Not every email needs the full CV.
12. At least one venue-true sentence per draft (flowers for a flower call, books for a bookshop, music/space for a café-label).

**G. Fact & consistency checks (pre-send gate)**
13. Subject and body must agree (edition numbers, years, program names). No stale years (no "2025" in a 2026 batch).
14. One venue = one email per batch (dedupe — biscuit gallery hit twice this round).
15. Render each venue's own name styling consistently within and across drafts (roman vs katakana).

**H. Close**
16. Warm, low-pressure, with a concrete ask (what she wants to know / can provide). Keep the Instagram line and leave a clean slot for name + portfolio link. Don't stack "Thank you for your time and consideration" on top of a second thank-you.
